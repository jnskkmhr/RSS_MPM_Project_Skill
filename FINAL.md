> **Condition on every MPM-sand result in this file (found 2026-11-25; `checks/T5.md` Check 3, `checks/T3.md` Checks 12 and 13, `checks/T2.md` Check 8).** In the code these results were measured on, the foot collision box carried a 2 cm contact margin in MPM sand environments (0 in the rigid and RFT tasks). With it the shin touches the foot and stops the ankle pitch at about -0.46 rad toes-up, 26 of the joint's 50 degrees; the real robot has no such stop. The MPM walking policy `aga5ovkp` and the MPM tow policy `her588wk` were trained with the stop on sand; the baselines never met it in training and met it in every MPM evaluation. The code is fixed since 2026-11-25 (the margin goes to the MPM solver only; `checks/T2_fix.md`) and the MPM walking policy is being retrained on it (W&B `j0scsgdy`). **Measured without the stop:** walking at +20, +25 and +30 degrees on beach sand and on level beach sand and loose poppy seed (slope section); towing with the tether cut and with 8 and 15 kg on beach sand and loose poppy seed (towing section). Removing it reverses the +30 degree result, changes no goal count at +20 and +25 degrees or on level sand, lowers the baselines' velocity error on level sand by 0.04 to 0.05 m/s, and changes how the MPM policy holds its foot and its torso. **Measured with the stop only and untested without it:** walking at +10 and +15 degrees and downhill, on the other sands of the material ladder and on the density-friction grid, with the sand kept awake, on the 0.65 m bed, on the planar ramp at +25 degrees; the whole-body, knee, swing-foot, steadiness, stance-foot-velocity and torque-limit analyses; the sand-flow volumes across materials; the tow sweeps beyond the cells of Check 8; the NeRD-to-MPM transfer numbers. Not at risk: T1 cost, T8 (box margin 0), T5 timings, results on rigid ground or RFT sand taken alone.

## Merged findings

### Q1 / T1: streaming MPM benchmark (merged 2026-11-19)

Conditions for every number: box probe (0.2 x 0.2 x 0.1 m, 10 kg), material `poppy_seed_loose`, voxel 0.03 m, sand depth 0.25 m, pool 1.2 x 1.2 m plus a 0.2 m ring, sparse grid, RTX 5090 (32 GB, also driving the desktop, about 1.7 GB in use), one run per configuration, seed 42, environment origin on the tile grid (a multiple of 0.2 m). The cache samples its sand per tile: 891 particles per 0.2 m tile, 15.5% more particles than the pool without cache holds for the same sand (57,024 against 49,379 in the pool), so the cost difference between the two streaming modes includes that sampling. Runs were made on IsaacLab `65093be1c` and Newton `6fee8b0b` with uncommitted changes, committed afterwards as IsaacLab `0013a0c2b` / `ddb9459f5` and Newton `7adb5be7`.

**Cost against terrain size** (corridor 1.6 m wide, one environment, 3.2 to 102.4 m):
- Stream without cache does not grow: 1,094 MiB and 16.6 to 17.0 ms per step at every length.
- Stream with cache grows only by its saved sand: 1,222 MiB and 17.0 ms at 3.2 m, 1,862 MiB and 19.3 ms at 102.4 m.
- Full grows with the terrain: 1,286 MiB and 19.6 ms at 3.2 m, 16,410 MiB and 309 ms at 102.4 m, which is 15 times the memory and 18 times the step time of stream without cache.
- Sleeping roughly halves full's step time on long terrain (164.9 against 309.1 ms at 102.4 m) and costs memory (21,146 against 16,410 MiB). On the streaming modes it adds up to about 1.8 ms per step and 66 to 98 MiB, and saves nothing.

**Cost against number of environments** (8 x 8 m of terrain per environment):
- Largest count that ran: full 4 (2 with sleeping); stream without cache 64; stream with cache 64 (32 with sleeping). Counts were doubled, so each limit lies between that count and the next.
- Per environment at the largest count: full 6,321 MiB and 126 ms; stream without cache 327 MiB and 5.2 ms; stream with cache 451 MiB and 5.8 ms.

**Terrain history** (box on a lemniscate, two laps, sleep off, cache with its tile template left unsettled, heights relative to each mode's own surface, box held at a fixed height):
- Height of the revisited track against full: 0.96 cm rms with the cache, which is the level of a repeated full run (0.83 cm); 4.97 cm median without the cache, where the track is lost.
- Drag on the revisited lap: full -26.7 N, with cache -29.4 N, without cache -54.3 N. The cache reproduces the drop in drag on revisit to within 10%; without it there is no drop.
- G1 on the same path: footprints are kept with the cache and lost without it. The percentages in the output depend on the reference level and are not merged.

> **On hold (2026-11-24, finding from T1, confirmed by `checks/T1.md`, check of 2026-11-24).** The box forces in this section are read at one moment of a 50 Hz cycle caused by the stepwise target of the box's gantry, and are lower than the mean force on the box (by a factor of about 1.6 to 2.5 in T1's rerun). The rerun is not a repeat of the merged runs: it ran at a 4 cm voxel (an uncommitted change of `VOXEL_SIZE` from 0.03 to 0.04 in the worktree), the merged runs at 3 cm. In that rerun, with the force taken at every physics step (one run per mode): the drag on the revisited lap falls to 0.50 of the first lap for full, 0.47 with the cache, and 1.02 without the cache, so the history result holds in kind. "The cache reproduces the drop in drag to within 10%" does not hold: the cache is 15% below full on the mean force. On fresh sand the cache is 8% below full and no cache 30% below. Absolute forces are not physical drag values. Not affected: memory, step time, environment counts, height maps, G1 runs.

**Caveats that limit what the paper can say:**
- **With sleeping on, the cache's force does not match full:** 31% low on fresh sand and 17% low on the revisited lap. The history result holds for sleep off only.
- **The history runs skip the cache's settled tile template**, by an override in the probe. The task as shipped uses the template, which makes fresh cached sand 43 to 57% stiffer in drag than a full bed.
- **Both streaming modes are softer than full on fresh sand:** 18 to 21% lower drag in the straight-drag debug check, 8 to 16% on the first lap.
- **Not from one commit.** The session reports that the commits differ from the run state only by formatting; that is not verified.
- **Not merged:** the comparison of grid types for full (unchecked; it suggests the fixed grid is a stronger full baseline, about 10 times the memory and 14 times the step time of stream without cache at 102.4 m instead of 15 and 18), and step times of the lemniscate runs.

The box is placed against each mode's own sand surface, measured after a 2 s hover: drag with the underside 6 cm below it at 0.5 m/s, press at 0.1 m/s with the force read at equal depth below the mode's own sand top. - **Cache against no cache:** fresh sand with the cache as shipped takes 2.4 and 2.35 times the press force at 2 and 6 cm (A; 2.2 and 2.1 at B) and 2.0 times the drag (2.8 at B). Its top is 2.2 cm lower (3.7 cm at B) and it is 10% denser; the mass of sand per unit area is the same in all modes within 1% (full at B 2% low). With the curves aligned at first contact the press ratio is 2.0 to 2.2. With sleeping on, as the trainings run, it is 1.8, 2.0 and 2.2 at 2, 4 and 6 cm (setting A, one run per mode).
- **Against full:** without cache the drag is 0.67 of full's at A and 0.39 at B; with the cache as shipped 1.34 and 1.11, and the press force at 6 cm 1.92 and 1.75. Full's bed keeps sinking during a run (0.7 cm between the placement of the box and the measured stretch at A), so its box is 0.5 to 0.7 cm shallower than in the streaming modes; with full's box at equal immersion (one run, A) the drag ratios are 0.57 without cache, 1.13 with the cache as shipped and 0.74 for the control. Not corrected at B.
- **Cause.** The settled template accounts for most of the cache's excess: left unsettled, the drag falls from -197 to -129 N (A) and from -291 to -138 N (B). The control still does not equal the pool without cache (1.30 and 1.34 times its drag). The cache also samples its sand per tile, with 25% (A) and 8% (B) more particles than the pool without cache; that and the mirrored tile are untested candidates. Why full is more resistant in drag than streamed sand of the same density is not identified (candidate: streamed sand reaches the body about a second after it enters the window).
- **How each mode prepares its sand (from the code).** Full: a jittered lattice, settled once (1 s in the task, the 2 s hover in this test), simulated throughout. Stream without cache: the same lattice, never settled; a reset and every strip entering the window start from the raw lattice. Stream with cache: every never-visited tile is a copy, mirrored on odd tiles, of one tile settled 2.5 s at start-up; visited tiles come back as they were left.
- **Limits.** A box, not a foot. Flat bed, nominal materials, no material randomization. Sleeping on was run for the press at setting A only. At B full ran on the fixed grid and the drag reaches a plateau only in the last 0.5 m. None of the three sands was compared with a real bed.
- **For the paper:** streaming MPM reproduces full-domain MPM's terrain memory at a fraction of its cost; it does not reproduce the resistance of full's fresh sand, and the two streaming variants differ from each other by a factor of two.

**The streaming window against the robot: its placement and its size (from T3's walking evaluation, `checks/T3.md` Checks 15 and 16).** *Placement, from the code.* With the tile cache on, the pool's centre is computed twice per step: `update_patch_center` snaps the anchor body to a 0.2 m grid anchored at the environment's origin, and `compute_tile_begins` replaces that centre by the nearest centre on the world tile grid; the replaced centre decides which particles are simulated. With r the remainder of the environment's origin against the tile, the window is shifted against the anchor by s = −r for r below half a tile and by one tile minus r above it; it steps at the same anchor positions for every r. Without the cache there is one snap and no shift. Every training environment has r = 0 (origins on tile centres of the 8 m terrain grid). In 16 cells of the walking evaluation the measured centre of the window equals the formula at every sample. *Shown, placement.* Policy trained on rigid ground, planar +15 degree ramp, beach sand, 0.02 m voxel, 1.2 m window with a 0.2 m ring, 40 trials per cell with identical starts in the lane's frame, 5 robots per run, one GPU pool: goals of 40 rise with s from 0 at s = −0.095 m through 20 to 26 at −0.015 m and 28 at +0.005 m to 36 to 39 at +0.085 and +0.095 m; two origins 0.01 m apart on either side of r = 0.1 m give 0 and 38; the pelvis height on the level approach follows s from −8.2 to +5.6 mm. By a reading committed before the cells were run the result follows the window's shift and not a 0.04 m period, the voxel phase or the lane's place on the tile grid. *Shown, size.* On identical starts (4 robots per run) the same policy has 0 walking goals of 40 with the 1.2 m window and 25 with a 1.6 m window at s = −0.015 m (31 and 36 goals), and 22 and 35 at s = +0.085 m; a walking goal is a goal at 0.7 to 1.3 times the commanded speed without sinking. With the 1.6 m window the placement no longer changes the pelvis height on level ground (+0.8 mm, p = 0.25) and still changes the climb (pelvis 40 mm higher at 5.4 m, p = 2e-5). The RFT-trained policy reaches 40 of 40 at both placements of the 1.2 m window; it was not run with the wider one. *From the code, bearing on the cause.* Ring particles have zero mass and enter the grid with infinite mass, so the ring is a boundary of zero velocity. With the task's sleeping the 1.2 m box around the pelvis does not keep sand awake; it limits where the robot's links may wake sand (0.62 m from the pelvis). Sand in motion stays simulated anywhere in the window until it has been quiet for 0.5 s, and quiet sand next to awake sand is itself a boundary of zero velocity. Sand enters the window at its front edge in the template's settled state and may sleep at once. *Not shown.* The cause (the ring ahead, the sleeping, or the time between sand entering the window and a foot reaching it). Which edge acts: the distances of simulated sand ahead of and behind the pelvis add up to a constant in the 1.2 m series; walking goals rise with the distance ahead over all cells (0.40 to 0.78 m) and are not ordered by the distance behind; the pelvis height on level ground is ordered by neither alone. The window size at which the result stops changing (1.6 m is the widest run). Any other policy, slope or sand, and the 0.04 m voxel of the trainings. Any effect on the memory or step times of this section. A box test by T1 (one run per cell, box 6 cm deep, 0.55 m or more from the ring) found no change in the drag across four origins, which does not test a body whose contacts come near the ring. *Robots in one run are not independent in the solver's control.* No sand passes between environments. With one environment `separate_worlds` is switched off (another grid allocation and stopping rule). With several, the stress and contact iteration stops for all of them when every environment has converged or at the cap of 25 iterations, and a tile change in any environment (with the cache) or any particle changing between ring and window (without it) discards the contact warm start of every environment: one solver step in six to nine in the 5-robot evaluation, of the order of every second step in a training with 32 sand environments (estimate). On 32 identical starts the pelvis rides 2.6 mm higher on the level approach with 4 robots per run than with 5 (p = 0.001; the two cells also differ in their code bundle). Whether the cap of 25 is reached in these runs is not recorded. *For this section it means:* the memory and step-time results stand. That a robot in the moving window behaves as on an unbounded bed is not shown, and for the 1.2 m window it fails in this test: at the limit of a policy the outcome of a walking task depends on the window's size, on a shift of its placement by centimetres and on how many environments share the solver. A statement that streaming does not change the outcome needs a window-size series that converges for the task, and the conditions of every benchmark should state the window, r and the number of environments.

**The iteration cap (from T3's walking evaluation and T1's box test; `checks/T3.md` Check 18; T1's test unchecked).** *From the code.* The sand solver's stress and contact iteration runs in passes of five sweeps until every environment of the run is under the tolerance (1e-4) or the sweep count passes `max_iterations`: 25 in the task and in every training. `eval_walk.py --max_iterations` changes that bound and nothing else; the tile template is settled by its own solver with 100 sweeps in every case. *The solve never reaches its tolerance.* Step time grows in proportion to the cap: 0.785 and 2.125 s per control step at 100 and 400 sweeps with 5 walking robots on the 0.02 m grid, a straight line from 25 to 200 in the training task (T1). No run records the sweeps or the residual. *What it does on the two grids (walking, tile cache on, the task's sleeping, 5 robots, identical starts, 40 paired trials).* At 0.02 m with a 0.50 m bed the feet sink 47 to 64 mm less on level beach sand at 100 sweeps than at 25, and 3 to 4 mm less again at 400. At 0.04 m with a 0.25 m bed, the trainings' grid, 5 to 6 mm less on beach sand and 8 to 19 mm on loose poppy seed; at 0.04 m with a 0.50 m bed and at 0.02 m with a 0.25 m bed 19 to 21 and 11 to 13 mm. The effect follows the number of cells through the bed (25, 12.5, 12.5, 6.25). T1's box agrees in direction: at 0.02 m the press force about doubles from 25 to 400 sweeps and still rises 22 to 34 % from 100 to 400; at the trainings' settings the drag is within 4 % and the press within 3 to 21 %. *Environments are coupled through it.* A pool change in any environment clears the contact warm start of all (11 % of solver steps with 2 walking robots, 21 to 26 % with 5 at +15 degrees, 22 to 63 % over the other cells), and all environments share the sweep count. With 25 sweeps the outcome depends on the number of robots per run (rigid-trained policy at +15 degrees: 21 walking goals of 40 with 2 robots, 0 with 4 or 5; pelvis 9.4 mm higher on level ground with 2). With 100 or 400 sweeps 2 and 5 robots do not differ in any paired measure. By a reading committed before the runs the cap carries the effect of the number of robots; whether through the cold starts or the sweep count is not separated. *Cost.* A training at 100 sweeps takes about 2.1 times the time per iteration and the same memory (T1, one trainer, 30 iterations). *Not shown.* Convergence at any cap for a robot on a slope; the sweep count needed on the trainings' sand without the tile cache; the effect on learning. Convergence in the sweep count is not convergence in the grid (T8). *For this section it means:* benchmark conditions must state the cap and the number of environments beside the window, and "environments are independent" holds for the sand, not for the solver's control, as long as the solve is cut.

### Q2 and Q3 / T2 and T3: G1 object towing (merged 2026-11-24)

Checked twice in `checks/T2.md` (first runs, then the rerun of 2026-11-24). Every number below reproduces from the raw trial files. Conditions: G1 tows a 15 kg, 0.3 m cube by a tether modelled as a one-sided spring and damper; three tow policies with one recipe, at their final checkpoints (W&B `aqiwzrv0` trained on rigid ground, `9733ux8d` on rigid ground and RFT, `her588wk` on rigid ground and MPM); 16 trials per cell (2 runs of 8, seeds 42 and 43); goal is 10 m of path; MPM sand is loose poppy seed (640 kg/m3, friction 0.73) on a 0.45 m bed, where no trial brings the feet within 2 cm of the rigid base; RFT sand has density 640 kg/m3, internal friction 0.73, and surface friction 0.73.

**The MPM-trained policy tows on MPM sand (T2).** Goals 16 of 16 at the 0.5 m/s and the 1.0 m/s straight commands. Forward velocity error 0.08 m/s at 0.5 m/s. At the 1.0 m/s command it does not reach the commanded speed: robot 0.69 m/s, cube 0.67 m/s, error 0.36 m/s. Training took 46.2 hours on 40 L40 GPUs.

**Sand resists the towed cube about twice as much as rigid ground (T3).** At a cube speed of about 0.47 m/s the horizontal pull is 103 N on MPM sand, about 50 N on rigid ground (42 to 55 N depending on heading), and 62 N on RFT sand (RFT-trained policy). The pull on MPM sand no longer changes with bed depth between 0.35 and 0.45 m at that speed.

**Goals reached out of 16, at 0.5 and 1.0 m/s straight (T3).**

| Tow policy | Rigid ground | RFT sand | MPM sand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trained on rigid ground | 16, 16 | 0, 0 | 0, 0 |
| Trained on rigid ground and RFT | 16, 16 | 16, 16 | 0, 0 |
| Trained on rigid ground and MPM | 16, 16 | 16, 14 | 16, 16 |

**Lean (T3).** For the MPM-trained policy, from rigid ground to the MPM sands the tether tension rises from 60 N to 93 to 136 N, the torso lean from about 8 degrees to 17 to 23 degrees, and the pelvis from 14 cm to 22 to 32 cm ahead of the stance foot.

**Caveats that limit what the paper can say:**
- **Compare forces at equal cube speed only.** "124 N against about 50 N at the 1.0 m/s command" compares a cube moving at 0.67 m/s with one at 0.99 m/s and is not merged. No policy moves the cube faster than 0.67 m/s on this sand.
- **The baselines' failure on MPM sand is not about the cube.** They fall within about 1.5 s of starting to walk, with or without the tether, and after standing still on the sand first. A follow-up run by the T2 session on the 0.45 m bed (unchecked, 16 trials per cell, tether cut) splits the cause: the RFT walking policy `6t0h2ud8` stays up in 16 of 16 trials at both speeds while the tow policy trained from it stays up in 0, so for RFT the tow training on flat ground removed walking on this sand; the rigid walking policy `3qymp37c` already stays up in only 6 of 16 at 0.5 m/s and 0 of 16 at 1.0 m/s, so for the rigid baseline the failure is mostly in the walking policy itself on deep loose poppy seed.
- **The tether-cut control is not a clean walking test for the MPM-trained policy at 1.0 m/s:** without the cube it stays up in 2 of 16 trials on the 0.45 m bed. The clean three-policy contrast is at 0.5 m/s.
- **The tow policy was trained on a 0.25 m bed** and from the earlier walking policy `eayrj79r`, not from `aga5ovkp`.
- **Recipe differences between the three tow policies** remain (mirror loss on for the two baselines and off for MPM, among others); see the differences table in `outputs/T3.md`.
- **Provenance:** two code states appear within single tables, and the evaluation scripts are not committed.
- **Not merged:** the lap (history) result, the turning rows, the start-up peak (1.02 to 1.11 times steady on the final checkpoint, not 1.15 to 1.21), and anything on the slides that still shows the earlier checkpoint.

**Sweep over sands from stiff to soft (merged 2026-11-24, `checks/T2.md` Check 3, pass with caveats).** Three tow policies, 15 kg cube, 0.5 m/s command, 16 trials per cell, rigid ground and 12 MPM sands (friction 0.7 and 0.9, densities 250 to 3000 kg/m3, and the named sands), with the cube and with the tether cut; 0.6 m bed, 4 cm voxel, sand kept awake, 12 s settling, start on each sand's settled surface. One sand left out (feet near the base).
- **The MPM-trained policy walks and tows on every sand:** 16 of 16 on all 12, with and without the cube.
- **The RFT-trained policy walks on the 7 stiffer sands and falls when the cube is attached.** With the tether cut 110 of 112 trials stay up; with the cube 12 of 112 reach the goal (8, 1, 2, 0, 1, 0, 0 of 16 per sand). It tows 16 of 16 on rigid ground (60 N) and on RFT sand (73 N); on these MPM sands it meets 83 to 94 N.
- **The rigid-trained policy stays up on none of the sands.**
- **Torso pitch of the MPM-trained policy rises with tension and then saturates.** 8.5 degrees at 60 N on rigid ground, 15 to 20 degrees at 83 to 106 N, 17 and 19 degrees at 126 and 140 N on the two softest sands, where the cube slows to 0.44 and 0.36 m/s. Rank correlation of tension and pitch +0.84 over 13 grounds.
- **Loose poppy seed in this sweep:** pull 94 N against the 103 N merged above (8 to 9% lower); pitch agrees.

Caveats:
- **"The RFT policy fails because sand resists more" is not shown.** Attaching the cube causes the falls, but the policy is already marginal on these sands without load: it walks at 0.63 to 0.71 m/s for a 0.5 m/s command with 13 to 15 degrees of torso pitch against 5.6 degrees on rigid ground. Its falls are forward pitch-overs; the tension is 55 to 70 N until 1.5 s before the fall. Goals fall with tension (rank correlation -0.95) but only over 83 to 92 N, and equally with sinkage and with overspeed. The missing control is a lighter cube on sand and a heavier cube on rigid ground or RFT sand.
- **The lean is not only a response to tension.** With the tether cut the MPM-trained policy's pitch already rises with softness (9 to 15 degrees); the extra pitch from the cube does not follow tension.
- **The baselines tow on too few grounds** (rigid 1, RFT 2) to say whether their pitch adapts less.

**Load control: is it the tension or the ground? (merged 2026-11-24, `checks/T2.md` Check 4, pass with caveats).** RFT-trained and MPM-trained tow policies, 0.5 m/s command, 16 trials per cell, same settings as the sweep; lighter cubes on the 7 stiffer MPM sands, heavier cubes on rigid ground and RFT sand.
- **The RFT-trained policy carries on other grounds the tension it falls under on MPM sand.** Rigid ground: 16 of 16 with 21 kg (83 N), 11 of 16 with 24 kg (95 N, the rest at the time limit); RFT sand: 16 of 16 with 18 kg (88 N) and with 20 kg (98 N); no fall in these 64 trials. MPM sands: 111 of 112 with 8 kg (41 to 47 N), 92 of 112 with 10 kg (53 to 60 N), 12 of 112 with 15 kg (83 to 94 N); every miss is a fall.
- **Its falls on MPM sand grow with the load,** on every sand; the step from 10 to 15 kg is clear everywhere.
- **Why it falls there:** on MPM sand it walks faster than commanded and pitched forward (cube at 0.66 to 0.80 m/s for a 0.5 m/s command and 21 to 28 degrees of torso pitch with the 8 and 10 kg cubes), so a tow load it carries elsewhere tips it over. Its start-up pelvis pitch peaks at 22 to 29 degrees without the cube, 31 to 38 degrees with 10 kg and 41 to 44 degrees with 15 kg, against a fall limit of 45 degrees. The tension before a fall is no higher than what it survives.
- **The MPM-trained policy tows every cube on every MPM sand,** 16 of 16, at 0.53 to 0.56 m/s with 14 to 17 degrees of pitch.
- **Not supported: "the RFT-trained policy fails because MPM sand needs more force than it can produce."** The data contradicts it as the mechanism.
