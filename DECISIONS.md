# Decisions

Append-only. Newest at the bottom. One entry per decision: date, decision, why.

- **2026-11-14. Evaluation is organized around five questions (Q1 to Q5).** See `tasks/README.md`. The earlier "gait style" question is folded into Q3 as the explanation of the performance gap, since a gait difference alone does not support a claim.
- **2026-11-14. Hardware is the primary comparison for Q3.** In-MPM evaluation favors MPM-trained policies by construction and is supporting evidence only.
- **2026-11-14. The rigid baseline uses randomized friction and contact compliance.** Reviewers will ask for a strong rigid baseline.
- **2026-11-14. Box pushing is the loco-manipulation task; object carrying is not added.** A carried object never touches the terrain, so carrying is walking with a payload and a reduced-order model handles it equally well. A pushed box ploughs, sinks, and leaves disturbed ground, which only particles represent.
- **2026-11-14. Particle NeRD sim-to-real is optional.** The paper must not depend on it.
- **2026-11-15. T1 builds on the main line (`junnosuke/mpm_rl_task`), not the experimental particle-sleeping line.** The paper's method (recycling patch and particle cache) and all trained policies live on the main line. Sleeping (Max's work, `jnskkmhr/experimental-mpm-sleeping` under `~/isaac/experimental/`, with forked Newton and Warp) keeps full particle storage, so it does not bound memory, and its checkouts have uncommitted changes. It is parked; it may be added later as one controlled row in the cost table.
- **2026-11-15. The IsaacLab working tree was committed as `dcdc2ff3c` on `junnosuke/mpm_rl_task`** (local only, not pushed), and the T1, T3, and T4 worktrees under `~/isaac/worktrees/` were fast-forwarded to it. `exports/` (1.4 GB of artifacts) stays untracked.
- **2026-11-15. Q1 is now the recycling-versus-caching question; simulation cost is no longer a question.** Bounded cost for a fixed-size patch is true by construction, so it is reported as a "Simulation Performance" paragraph with numbers. The question list stays Q1 to Q5 and Q2 to Q5 are unchanged. Handoffs written before this date use the old Q1 wording (cost, fidelity, and cache together); T1 still covers all of that work.
- **2026-11-16. Q3 is a comparison among the baselines, in simulation on terrain beyond the training distribution and on hardware.** The question now asks how MPM-trained policies compare with rigid and reduced-order ones, instead of asking whether MPM improves sim-to-real transfer. `tasks/T3.md` gains a defined out-of-distribution experiment: a sand slope-angle sweep from inside the training range to beyond it, plus held-out terrain shapes, with held-out material parameters, for all four policies. Why: sand slopes are where rigid and reduced-order baselines are most likely to separate from MPM, and a neutral question still stands if the baselines match. Hardware stays the primary comparison (2026-11-14); the simulation sweep is supporting evidence. A sloped bed on hardware is optional. The slope range, training limit, and terrain shapes are not decided here; the T3 session takes them from the training configurations.
- **2026-11-16. T1 uses a primitive probe only, and the policy-level cache comparison is dropped.** The probe is a rigid box on a PD-driven gantry with no robot and no policy; the three modes are full-domain, recycling, and recycling with cache. Why: T1 evaluates the terrain representation, and a prescribed trajectory lets force and sinkage be compared across modes without gait divergence (the same policy and seed do not give the same robot motion). The policy-level comparison needs a robot and training, so Q1 answers whether the terrain response differs, not whether a trained policy is affected. Patch-size, boundary-width, and discretization ablations are also dropped from T1. GPU utilization is added to the cost metrics. Agreed with the T1 session on 2026-11-15 (`outputs/T1.md` section A); the paper already describes this experiment.
- **2026-11-18. T1 is a numerical benchmark of streaming MPM with adaptive particle activation (sleeping), run on the unified MPM code.** This replaces the 2026-11-15 decision that parked sleeping: it has been ported into the unified code (`~/isaac/worktrees/mpm-unification/IsaacLab`, branch `jnskkmhr/mpm-unification`), so it no longer needs the separate experimental stack. Sleeping is presented with streaming as part of the method, not as a separate contribution, and strain and region activation are reported as one mechanism. T1 asks three questions: scaling with terrain size, scaling with the number of environments, and preserving terrain history. Six modes: sleep off or on, times full, stream without cache, and stream with cache. The box probe is used for every number; G1 on a lemniscate is added for terrain history as height maps and video. Terrain-size scaling uses a corridor as wide as the patch, with `length_x` on the x-axis, because a square terrain would overstate full-domain's cost for a one-directional push. Environment scaling uses the training terrain size. The earlier box results from `IsaacLab-T1` were run on the old code and are for planning only.
- **2026-11-18. T2 covers three embodiments and three tasks; the box is no longer the pushed object.** Embodiments: a manipulator, a quadruped (Go2), and the G1 humanoid. Tasks: manipulation of granular material, walking, and object pushing. This settles the open quadruped question: Go2 is included, for walking, in simulation only. Objects for G1 pushing are the drum and the chair, and the table if time allows. This replaces the box in the 2026-11-14 decision: on sand the box tips before it slides at G1 wrist height (`outputs/T2.md`, section 11, unchecked). Object pushing is still the loco-manipulation task, and carrying is still out. Hardware is G1 only; the manipulator on hardware is optional and low priority. - **2026-11-18. T3 and T4 are merged into one question: when does training on an explicit particle simulation help?** Three axes: rougher terrain (generalization beyond the training slopes and shapes), softer material (snow-like, powdery, sticky), and terrain interaction (object pushing). The walking comparison against the baselines is the measurement for each axis, not a result of its own. T4 is retired and its number is not reused. Policies: MJWarp-Rough (rigid, default MuJoCo Warp contact, randomized foot friction), RFT-Rough, and MPM-Rough; a rigid policy with randomized contact stiffness and damping is optional. This replaces the 2026-11-14 decision that the rigid baseline must randomize contact compliance; the paper then names the baseline as standard rigid-terrain training and does not describe it as compliant contact. Hardware (G1 on real sand, baselines included) stays the deciding comparison for the terrain and pushing axes; the soft-material axis is simulation only, with hardware tests on snow or a dune as an option if the chance comes. The slope sweep of 2026-11-16 is now part of the rougher-terrain axis. Dropped: the MPM-Flat policy, policies trained on nominal-only or narrow material ranges, particle resolution, and the six gait metrics as a separate result (one explanatory measurement per axis instead). T3 moves to the unified MPM code, with all compared policies trained from one commit.
- **2026-11-19. The pushed object for G1 is chosen by a force probe, and no object is ruled out beforehand.** This replaces the part of the 2026-11-18 T2 entry that fixed the drum and the chair and dropped the box. Candidates are the box, sphere, and cylinder of the loco-manipulation debug task in the unified code and the drum and chair from the old code. The bed is sampled at about 1.25 particles per cell edge, compacts about 15% after generation, and keeps sinking while awake; sleeping freezes it mid-compaction.
