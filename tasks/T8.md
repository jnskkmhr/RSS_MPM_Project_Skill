- **Paper target:** an additional section on the accuracy of MPM for granular terrain

## Goal
Readers will ask whether MPM can be trusted for granular terrain. Answer it by running the same box drag test in a discrete element simulation (DEM), which resolves individual grains, and comparing it with MPM: how the particles deform, how they move, and the force on the box.

## Steps
1. **Environment.** Get Chrono DEM-Engine running on OSMO (GPU). Record the image, build steps, and a smoke test.
2. **Reference test.** Take the MPM box drag test as it was run (T1's box probe: a rigid box moved along a prescribed path through the sand; T3's level-cube drag if useful) and fix one test definition: box size and mass, depth, speed, path, bed size and depth.
3. **Material.** Align the DEM material with the MPM material. MPM is a continuum with a bulk density and an internal friction; DEM has grain density, grain size and shape, and grain-to-grain friction. Match the bulk behaviour, by calibration: settled bulk density, and the bulk friction (for example the angle at which a pile or a tilted bed gives way) in both simulators. State every parameter on both sides and what could not be matched.
4. **Run** the box drag in DEM with that material, and the matching MPM run if the existing MPM data lacks a quantity.
5. **Compare** MPM and DEM.

## Acceptance criteria

| # | Question | Metrics | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Is the material the same in both? | Bulk density after settling, bulk friction (pile or tilt angle), grain size against box size, for MPM and DEM side by side | Step 3 |
| P2 | Does the sand deform the same way? | Surface height map after the drag (trench and pile), and its difference between MPM and DEM | Steps 4, 5 |
| P3 | Does the sand move the same way? | Particle velocity field around the box in a vertical slice, at the same instants | Steps 4, 5 |
| P4 | Is the force on the box the same? | Horizontal and vertical force on the box against time or distance, MPM and DEM, with the ratio | Steps 4, 5 |

Measurement rules:
1. **Same test in both:** box, depth below the settled surface, speed, path, bed dimensions. List every difference that remains.
2. **Depths from the settled surface**, measured in each run.
3. **The bed must be deep and wide enough** that the box and the moving sand do not feel the floor or the walls; say how this was checked.
4. **DEM settings stated:** grain size and count, shape, contact model and its parameters, time step, how the bed was prepared.
5. **Conditions** for every result: code version, command, seed, GPU, OSMO workflow, and the number of runs.
6. **Neither simulator is the truth.** Report agreement and disagreement; do not tune one to match the other beyond the material calibration in step 3.

## Round 3 (added 2026-11-24, at Junnosuke's request)
The MPM runs so far used the locomotion task's discretization (3 cm voxel, about 2 particles per cell, 5 ms step, 25 iterations), which was not tuned for this test. Newton's soil benchmark (<https://claude.ai/artifact/STtGSyiJRiQRXtAZxnE8V5>, single wheel W1 and column collapse F1) uses a much finer setup: 20 to 40 cells across the body, 8 material points per cell with a randomised layout (jitter 0.5 spacing), sub-millisecond steps, a 250-iteration limit, tolerance 1e-4 to 1e-5, APIC.

6. **Rerun the MPM side with a discretization tuned for the test**, following that benchmark, and see whether the comparison with DEM changes. The DEM results are kept as they are; no new DEM runs unless a number is missing.

| # | Question | Metrics | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| P5 | Does a finer, well-sampled MPM change the comparison with DEM? | Drag force against voxel size down to the finest that fits, at 8 points per cell, with step size and iteration limit scaled; whether the force converges; trench, berm, moving sand and tilt angle at the finest setting, beside the unchanged DEM values and the task-setting values | Step 6 |

Rules for round 3: the task-setting MPM results stay on the slides beside the tuned ones, since the policies train at task settings. Check at each resolution that the box reaches its depth before the averaging window and state the pressing force. Repeat the finest setting at least three times with different beds and grid alignments.

## Round 5: several motion types, statistics over runs (added 2026-11-24, at Junnosuke's request)
A single box drag is one kind of sand interaction. The robot tasks load the sand in three ways, so the comparison covers all three and reports statistics over repeated runs, not one snapshot.

Motion types, same rigid body in MPM and DEM (fixed by Junnosuke 2026-11-24): four, reported separately, with no combined motions.
1. **Horizontal push** (the drag so far).
2. **Push downward:** press the body into the sand to a set depth.
3. **Push upward:** lift the body out of the sand from that depth.
4. **Twist:** the body at a set depth turned about the vertical axis.

Each motion at more than one speed and depth, with repeats (different beds or seeds, and grid alignments in MPM), in both MPM settings (task settings and the tuned grid).

| # | Question | Metrics | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| P6 | Does MPM deform the sand as DEM does across the motion types the robot uses? | Per motion type, mean and spread over runs of: correspondence of the surface height change (correlation and RMS difference of the height-change maps, trench or pit depth, rim or berm height); displacement error of the sand by segment (the bed divided into cells; mean displacement vector of the sand in each cell, MPM against DEM, as RMS error and as a fraction of the DEM displacement); the same DEM seed-against-seed as the noise floor | Round 5 |

Forces are reported only with the corrected recording (target updated every physics step, mean over physics steps).

## Round 8: material matched by element tests, after Agarwal, Goldman and Kamrin (added 2026-11-25, at Junnosuke's request; plan only, nothing run)
Reference: Agarwal, Goldman, Kamrin, "Mechanistic framework for reduced-order models in soft materials: application to three-dimensional granular intrusion", arXiv:2205.14920 (main text `~/RSS_27/rft_dem.pdf`, supplement `~/isaac/pnas.2214017120.sapp.pdf`, both read 2026-11-25). They fix the DEM material, measure the continuum parameters from it with element tests, set the continuum model to those values, and compare without further tuning. Their DEM (LIGGGHTS, supplement S8, Table S2): 3 and 3.4 mm spheres (50/50), grain density 2470 kg/m3, Hookean contact, E = 7e6 Pa, Poisson 0.45, restitution 0.88, grain-grain friction 0.05, grain-wall friction 0.4, bulk density 1310 kg/m3 (volume fraction 0.53 in the text, packing about 0.59 in Table S2);  internal friction mu_int = 0.21 from simple-shear DEM, not from the contact friction (0.05) or a repose angle (smooth spheres at friction 0.05 give a bulk friction of 0.21, so contact friction and bulk friction differ by a factor of four); the shear test is a plane-strain bed of 100d x 50d x 90d sheared between two rigid plates, a constant force on the top plate (constant pressure P = F_top/A), the bottom plate moved in x, mu_int = tau/P = F_shear/F_top from the plateau of the time series (Fig S6), periodic in y; intruders 16 to 30 grains across; quasi-static checked with the inertial number I < 0.01 and the macro-inertial number Imac < 0.15.

Rounds 1 to 7 did it the other way: DEM material A was tuned to the task-setting MPM sand by the tilt angle (32.5 deg, 1 cm/s rule) and the settled bulk density. The tilt test is a free-surface failure test, not an element test, and the MPM sliding angle depends on the discretization (32.5 deg at task settings, 40 deg with dense sampling, preset 36 deg), so the MPM friction is an outcome and not an input. Round 8 removes the tuning and says which parameters are matched and how.

Supplement, usable here: the force scale of the continuum model is xi_n = rho_c g (894 mu^3 - 386 mu^2 + 89 mu) (Fig S4, Table S1, fitted at rho_c = 3000 kg/m3; the dependence on rho_c is linear, that on mu_int is superlinear). It predicts how much an error in the friction angle changes the drag force, which gives a check on P4 independent of the DEM runs: for example mu_int = tan 32.5 deg = 0.64 against tan 36 deg = 0.73 against tan 40 deg = 0.84 changes xi_n by the ratio of the cubic (to be computed in step 9, not quoted here). It applies to a continuum with a non-dilatant flow rule and a rate-independent response, the same as the MPM preset; the paper warns that it is less accurate near sharp corners and for objects not much larger than the grains, and fits plates with depth up to about ten times the intruder size.

7. **Keep DEM material A unchanged** (see the minimal-change principle below) and state that it is a reference material, not a real sand (material identification for the real terrain is a proposed task). Their grains have a contact friction of 0.05 and are soft (E 7e6 Pa) and spherical; ours are three-sphere clumps with contact friction 0.5 (A) or 0.8 (B), so bulk friction here comes from shape and contact friction together; the shear test decides. Grain size relative to the box (13 grains across at 15 mm) against the paper's 16 to 30; the DEM force was not converged in grain size (-80 to -61 N from 20 to 9 mm), so state the grain size at which the force is converged or its remaining sensitivity.
8. **Element test in DEM:** simple shear (constant normal stress, then constant volume if the MPM model allows) on a periodic box of the same grains, giving mu_int, the critical (close-packed) and settled bulk density, and the wall friction of the box and floor surfaces. Quasi-static: report I.
9. **The same element test in MPM**, at the discretizations used (task settings, the 1 cm fine grid, and the dense sampling of P9): the friction angle the MPM sand shows at the stress levels of the drag, and its volume change. Parameters the continuum model has that the DEM test has to determine or confirm: friction angle, critical density, dilatancy, hardening, yield pressure (the preset sets the last three to zero or very large; state what DEM shows at the same stress, a dilating sand is not the same as a non-dilatant model).
10. **Set MPM to the DEM values and compare without tuning.** Where the MPM friction is an outcome of the discretization, report the discretization at which it matches, and the cost, instead of changing DEM. Repeat the four motions and the fixed-weight press of rounds 5 to 6 against the unchanged DEM. Check for every case: I < 0.01 in both, Imac < 0.15, grains across the box, and the quasi-static assumption for the press.
11. **Parameters that cannot be matched** (grain stiffness, restitution, rolling resistance, shape, which a rate-independent continuum model does not have) are listed with a test of their influence in DEM (as in `t8-dem-sens-1`).

| # | Question | Metrics | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| P11 | Do MPM and DEM show the same bulk material under an element test? | mu_int, critical and settled density, wall friction, volume change in simple shear, DEM against MPM at each discretization; I | Steps 8 and 9 |
| P12 | With the material matched by element tests and not by the tilt test, do deformation, sand motion and force agree? | The metrics of P2 to P6 and the press of P8, against the unchanged DEM | Step 10 |

Minimal-change principle (Junnosuke, 2026-11-25): rounds 1 to 7 and their slides stay as they are; round 8 only adds. DEM material A (three-sphere clumps, 15 mm, friction 0.5, grain density 1196 kg/m3, E and restitution as run) is kept unchanged as the reference material, and is not replaced by the paper's grains. It is measured by the element test, not re-tuned. On the MPM side the fewest parameters are changed: first the friction angle of the preset (the one input that sets mu_int), at the discretization under test, and nothing else (sampling, voxel, step, iterations, density, box and wall friction, gantry stay as in rounds 5 to 7). Only if friction alone does not bring the shear-test result within the criterion is a second parameter named, with the reason, before it is changed. Old and new MPM results are shown side by side, labelled, and the old ones are not overwritten.

Rules for round 8: write the test definitions, the measures and what counts as matched (for example mu_int within 0.02) into `outputs/T8.md` before any run; no parameter of either simulator is changed after seeing the comparison, except the single one named in step 10 for the MPM side. The tilt test is kept as an independent check of both, not as the calibration. Slides: one section "Material matching", with the justification from the paper in two lines.
