- **Owner:** lead-launched agent (started 2026-11-24, overnight, at Junnosuke's request)
- **Status:** rounds 1 and 2 done and checked (2026-11-24); round 3 (tuned MPM discretization) in progress
- **Depends on:** the MPM box test from T1 (and T3's box drag) as the reference to reproduce
- **Repo:** new workspace `~/isaac/dem_compare/` for the DEM code and comparison scripts (new files only). MPM side: the unified code in `~/isaac/worktrees/mpm-unification/IsaacLab`, used as it is. DEM: Chrono DEM-Engine, <https://github.com/projectchrono/DEM-Engine>, run on OSMO.
- **Paper target:** an additional section on the accuracy of MPM for granular terrain

## Goal
Readers will ask whether MPM can be trusted for granular terrain. Answer it by running the same box drag test in a discrete element simulation (DEM), which resolves individual grains, and comparing it with MPM: how the particles deform, how they move, and the force on the box.

## Steps
1. **Environment.** Get Chrono DEM-Engine running on OSMO (GPU). Record the image, build steps, and a smoke test.
2. **Reference test.** Take the MPM box drag test as it was run (T1's box probe: a rigid box moved along a prescribed path through the sand; T3's level-cube drag if useful) and fix one test definition: box size and mass, depth, speed, path, bed size and depth.
3. **Material.** Align the DEM material with the MPM material. MPM is a continuum with a bulk density and an internal friction; DEM has grain density, grain size and shape, and grain-to-grain friction. Match the bulk behaviour, by calibration: settled bulk density, and the bulk friction (for example the angle at which a pile or a tilted bed gives way) in both simulators. State every parameter on both sides and what could not be matched.
4. **Run** the box drag in DEM with that material, and the matching MPM run if the existing MPM data lacks a quantity.
5. **Compare** MPM and DEM.

## Acceptance criteria

| # | Question | Metrics | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Is the material the same in both? | Bulk density after settling, bulk friction (pile or tilt angle), grain size against box size, for MPM and DEM side by side | Step 3 |
| P2 | Does the sand deform the same way? | Surface height map after the drag (trench and pile), and its difference between MPM and DEM | Steps 4, 5 |
| P3 | Does the sand move the same way? | Particle velocity field around the box in a vertical slice, at the same instants | Steps 4, 5 |
| P4 | Is the force on the box the same? | Horizontal and vertical force on the box against time or distance, MPM and DEM, with the ratio | Steps 4, 5 |

Measurement rules:
1. **Same test in both:** box, depth below the settled surface, speed, path, bed dimensions. List every difference that remains.
2. **Depths from the settled surface**, measured in each run.
3. **The bed must be deep and wide enough** that the box and the moving sand do not feel the floor or the walls; say how this was checked.
4. **DEM settings stated:** grain size and count, shape, contact model and its parameters, time step, how the bed was prepared.
5. **Conditions** for every result: code version, command, seed, GPU, OSMO workflow, and the number of runs.
6. **Neither simulator is the truth.** Report agreement and disagreement; do not tune one to match the other beyond the material calibration in step 3.

## Round 3 (added 2026-11-24, at Junnosuke's request)
The MPM runs so far used the locomotion task's discretization (3 cm voxel, about 2 particles per cell, 5 ms step, 25 iterations), which was not tuned for this test. Newton's soil benchmark (<https://claude.ai/artifact/STtGSyiJRiQRXtAZxnE8V5>, single wheel W1 and column collapse F1) uses a much finer setup: 20 to 40 cells across the body, 8 material points per cell with a randomised layout (jitter 0.5 spacing), sub-millisecond steps, a 250-iteration limit, tolerance 1e-4 to 1e-5, APIC.

6. **Rerun the MPM side with a discretization tuned for the test**, following that benchmark, and see whether the comparison with DEM changes. The DEM results are kept as they are; no new DEM runs unless a number is missing.

| # | Question | Metrics | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| P5 | Does a finer, well-sampled MPM change the comparison with DEM? | Drag force against voxel size down to the finest that fits, at 8 points per cell, with step size and iteration limit scaled; whether the force converges; trench, berm, moving sand and tilt angle at the finest setting, beside the unchanged DEM values and the task-setting values | Step 6 |

Rules for round 3: the task-setting MPM results stay on the slides beside the tuned ones, since the policies train at task settings. Check at each resolution that the box reaches its depth before the averaging window and state the pressing force. Repeat the finest setting at least three times with different beds and grid alignments.

## Round 5: several motion types, statistics over runs (added 2026-11-24, at Junnosuke's request)
A single box drag is one kind of sand interaction. The robot tasks load the sand in three ways, so the comparison covers all three and reports statistics over repeated runs, not one snapshot.

Motion types, same rigid body in MPM and DEM:
1. **Horizontal push** (the drag so far).
2. **Vertical stamp and pull-out:** press the body into the sand to a set depth, hold, lift it out.
3. **Twist:** the body at a set depth turned about the vertical axis (yaw rate above zero), alone and combined with a horizontal push.

Each motion at more than one speed and depth, with repeats (different beds or seeds, and grid alignments in MPM), in both MPM settings (task settings and the tuned grid).

| # | Question | Metrics | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| P6 | Does MPM deform the sand as DEM does across the motion types the robot uses? | Per motion type, mean and spread over runs of: correspondence of the surface height change (correlation and RMS difference of the height-change maps, trench or pit depth, rim or berm height); displacement error of the sand by segment (the bed divided into cells; mean displacement vector of the sand in each cell, MPM against DEM, as RMS error and as a fraction of the DEM displacement); the same DEM seed-against-seed as the noise floor | Round 5 |

Forces are reported only with the corrected recording (target updated every physics step, mean over physics steps).
