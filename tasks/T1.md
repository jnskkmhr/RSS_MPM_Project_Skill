## Goal
Show that the moving patch keeps memory and time bounded regardless of traversed distance, that it reproduces full-domain MPM contact physics, and that the particle cache restores deformed terrain on revisits.

## Starting points
- Recycling patch: `IsaacContrib-Velocity-Sand-G1-29dof-MPM-MovingPatch`
- Cached patch: `IsaacContrib-Velocity-Sand-G1-29dof-MPM-MovingPatch-ParticleCache`
- Full-domain reference: `contrib/velocity_mpm/g1_29dof_mpm` is the candidate; confirm it simulates the whole terrain, otherwise build one.

## Steps
1. **Cost.** For full-domain, recycling, and cached modes, sweep `num_envs`, patch size, and traversed distance (or terrain size) with matched material, particle spacing, timestep, and robot trajectories. Run full-domain up to the largest configuration that fits in memory and record where it fails.
2. **Fidelity.** Replay identical robot trajectories in all three modes; compare foot reaction forces and foot sinkage against full-domain.
3. **Cache.** Walk a closed trajectory that returns to the robot's own footprints; compare terrain height and foot forces on the revisit across the three modes.
4. **Cache at policy level.** Train with recycling and with cached patches and evaluate on a task with revisits (scenario still open, see `DECISIONS.md`).

## Acceptance criteria
1. Plots of peak VRAM, wall-clock time, and physics time per step per environment against `num_envs`, for all three modes on the same GPU.
2. The same quantities against traversed distance or terrain size, showing whether patch cost stays flat.
3. Force and sinkage error against full-domain, with the trajectory and material stated.
4. Revisit comparison: height-map and force difference for recycling vs. cached vs. full-domain.
5. Policy-level cache comparison, or a written statement that it showed no difference.
6. Timing includes host-side tile paging and synchronization; excludes rendering and warm-up.
