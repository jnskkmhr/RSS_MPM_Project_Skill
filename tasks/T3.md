## Goal
Answer one question at the policy level: when does training on an explicit particle simulation help, compared with training on rigid terrain or a reduced-order terrain model?

Two axes (merged from three on 2026-11-23):
- **A. Yielding terrain.** Slope and sand material are two ways of making the ground give way more. Statement to test: training on flowing sand keeps policies stable on steep slopes.
  - H1. On slopes the sand under a loaded foot moves downhill, and it moves more as the slope gets steeper or the sand gets softer or more slippery.
  - H2. The policy trained on MPM copes with this better than MJWarp and RFT.
- **B. Terrain interaction.** In object towing, what is different about towing on MPM sand, and does the policy apply the force the soil requires?

This is a comparison, not a claim that MPM wins everywhere. H1 and H2 are hypotheses: report plainly if the data does not support them. Where particles do not help (flat and gently sloped ground, hard ground, and possibly downhill) is reported inside axis A.

## Steps
1. **Evaluate the final checkpoint of MPM-Rough** on the slope and material tests, with MJWarp-Rough and RFT-Rough, and say plainly where it is better and worse than the baselines and than the earlier MPM policy.
2. **Axis A, results.** Terrain geometry and terrain material are varied separately, one at a time (decided 2026-11-23).
   - **Geometry.** Success rate and tracking error against slope angle, uphill and downhill, on the firm training sand.
   - **Material.** On level ground and a mild slope, vary the material parameters from firm to soft and report how each policy degrades. Flat, hard ground is the easy end.
   - The slope-by-friction grid of 2026-11-23, which combined steep slopes with out-of-range materials, is kept as a stated limit and is not the material result.
3. **Axis A, explanation.**
   - Foot motion during contact, split into the foot sliding over the sand and the sand under the foot moving with it; volume of sand displaced per step.
   - Foot behaviour: stance duration, step length, step time, foot pitch at touchdown, and how quickly load comes onto the foot.
   - A close-up side view of feet and particles on a slope for each policy, the trial chosen by rule (the median trial at a fixed angle), with counts of failure types per policy.
4. **Axis A, sanity checks for the downhill result.** State the sand used and its friction; fix and run the rigid-slope downhill evaluation; measure the sand's own sliding angle by tilting an unloaded bed, and express each slope relative to it; compare how much sand moves under a stance foot downhill against uphill.
5. **Axis B.** Towing on rigid ground, RFT sand with the MPM sand's density and friction, and MPM sand, for the three towing policies, with the tether-cut control.
6. **Hardware.** G1 on real sand, trials in randomized order. On flat ground and a 10 degree slope every policy succeeded, so the next test is a slope of about 20 degrees.

Optional, if time allows: sideways, diagonal, and circular walking on slopes, and terrain shapes none of the policies trained on.

## Acceptance criteria

| # | Question | Metrics | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Yielding terrain: does training on flowing sand keep the policy stable as the ground gives way more? | (a) Geometry: success rate and velocity tracking error against slope angle, uphill and downhill, on firm sand. Material: the same, with step time, on level ground and a mild slope as the material goes from firm to soft, including hard ground where the policies do not differ. (b) Sand motion under the stance foot against slope angle and friction (H1). (c) Foot slip split into sliding over the sand and moving with it, and foot behaviour per policy (H2). (d) Failure types per policy, with a close-up of feet and particles | Steps 1 to 4 |
| P2 | Terrain interaction: what is different about towing on MPM sand, and does the policy apply the force the soil requires? | (a) Ground force on the towed object and tether tension on rigid ground, material-matched RFT sand, and MPM sand. (b) Whether displaced sand and earlier deformation change the force. (c) Success rate and goal reaching for the three towing policies, with the tether-cut control | Step 5 |
| P3 | Does the result hold on hardware? | Success rate, velocity tracking, and step time per policy and slope; trials per cell, every failure described | Step 6 |

Measurement rules:
1. **Hardware is the deciding comparison.** Evaluating in MPM favors the policy trained in MPM, so P1 and P2 are supporting evidence. If the baselines match MPM on hardware, report that plainly; on flat ground and at 10 degrees they did.
2. **Hypotheses are tested, not illustrated.** For H1 and H2, report the measurement whichever way it comes out. Fix grids and choose example trials by rule before looking at results.
3. **Depths are measured from the settled sand surface,** not from the level the ground is generated at.
4. **Name the rigid baseline for what it is:** standard rigid-terrain training with friction randomization.
5. **Recipes and budgets.** List every difference between the compared policies, including total training transitions. The three towing policies share one recipe.
6. **Towing needs the tether-cut control.** A policy that falls with the tether cut fails at walking, and its towing failure says nothing about the force on the object.
7. **Failed episodes.** Success rate covers every episode. Quantities averaged over successful trials only say so, with the number of trials behind each point.
8. **Conditions** for every result: task ID, commit, run ID, checkpoint, material, terrain, commands, seeds, trial count, GPU.
