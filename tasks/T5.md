# T5: Particle NeRD speedup and transfer (Q5)

- **Owner:** TODO
- **Status:** todo
- **Depends on:** an MPM-trained reference policy on the same terrain type (T3)
- **Repo:** IsaacLab, `junnosuke/particle_nerd`
- **Paper target:** the four Q5 paragraphs in `sections/evaluation.tex`

## Goal
Show whether training with Particle NeRD is faster than training in MPM once data generation and network training are counted, and whether the resulting policy works in MPM. Hardware transfer is optional.

## Starting points
- Read `examples/particle_nerd/agent/README.md` and `examples/particle_nerd/documentation/methodology.rst` first.
- G1 pipeline: `examples/particle_nerd/g1/` (collection, training, closed-loop evaluation)
- NeRD-backed task: `IsaacContrib-Velocity-Sand-G1-29dof-ParticleNeRD-MovingPatch`
- MPM counterpart: `IsaacContrib-Velocity-Sand-G1-29dof-MPM-MovingPatch`

## Steps
1. **Accuracy.** Particle displacement error and body wrench error against MPM as a function of rollout horizon, on held-out trajectories.
2. **Cost.** Throughput and peak VRAM against MPM at matched particle count on the same GPU. Record data-generation and network-training cost.
3. **Policy.** Train a locomotion policy in NeRD and one in MPM within the same material range; plot learning curves against wall-clock time.
4. **Sim-to-sim.** Evaluate the NeRD-trained policy in MPM against the MPM-trained policy.
5. **Hardware (optional).** Deploy the NeRD-trained policy on the T3 test bed.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Rollout error plot for particle displacement and body wrench against horizon.
2. Throughput and VRAM table against MPM; timing covers feature construction, network inference, contact reconstruction, rigid-body integration, and patch updates.
3. Break-even point: training time after which NeRD's up-front cost is recovered.
4. Learning curves against wall-clock time for both simulators.
5. Success rate and tracking errors in MPM for the NeRD-trained and MPM-trained policies.
6. The terrain type and material range NeRD was trained on, stated explicitly.
