## 4. Cache or no cache: what the NeRD counterpart is

- MPM without cache (`aga5ovkp`): a fixed pool of 20,000 particles per environment wraps around the pelvis; a
  strip that leaves at the back reappears at the front as fresh sand on the terrain; the ring is kinematic.
- MPM with cache (`cfbj8jpx`): tiles that leave are saved and restored on return, so footprints persist.
- **NeRD counterpart of no cache (built, `nerd_streaming.py`):** the same wrapping rule and the same pool. A
  recycled particle gets its site position, zero velocity and a constant three-frame position history (the
  network's whole particle state). Ring particles are pinned. The tray of the network moves with the pool, and
  predicted positions are kept above the support surface by a ray query on the terrain. With `active_k`, only
  the 1,536 particles nearest the feet (and fastest) are stepped; the rest keep their place, which plays the role
  the MPM sleeping preset plays in `aga5ovkp`.
- **NeRD counterpart of the cache: not built, not launched.** Newton's tile cache is written against the MPM
  solver (spare particle slots, per-tile records of MPM state, a template settled by MPM steps). The neural solver
  assumes the same particle count in every environment and has no notion of empty slots. A cache for it needs a
  new record store and a masked particle set in the solver; that is not a cheap addition. It would also only
  matter when a robot walks back over its own footprints within a 20 s episode.

**Why the NeRD sand does not yield** (asked by the lead on 2026-11-25; existing data and two more rollouts, no
retraining). Same policy (MPM `aga5ovkp`, iteration 19,999), commands and seed in both simulators, 16 environments
(8 on sand), 500 steps, sand 1600 kg/m³ and friction 0.48; `nerd_smoke.py --trace`, `fidelity_report.py`;
`logs/t5/fidelity/trace_{nerd,mpm}.npz`, `logs/t5/fig/fidelity_summary.json`; figure `site/T5/fig/fidelity_force_depth.png`.

| Vertical sand force on the feet | Particle NeRD | MPM |
|---|---|---|
| Mean over the run, both feet [N] | 349 | 338 |
| Mean on a loaded foot (above 20 N) [N] | 258 | 267 |
| 95th percentile on a loaded foot / largest [N] | 483 / 1,208 | 407 / 712 |
| Mean force with the foot origin 2 to 3 cm above the surface (sole about at the surface) [N] | 284 | 74 |
| Mean force with the foot origin 4 to 5 cm above the surface (sole 1 cm above it) [N] | 227 | 33 |
| Mean force with the foot origin 5 to 6 cm below the surface [N] | 182 | 254 |
| Median height of the foot origin while loaded [m] | +0.018 | -0.058 |

- **The vertical force is right on average (it has to be: both carry the robot's weight) and wrong in where it
  acts.** NeRD delivers the full stance force with the sole at the undisturbed surface and already 227 N with the
  sole 1 cm above it; MPM gives 74 N at the surface and builds the force up over 6 to 10 cm of sinkage. NeRD's
  peaks are higher (1,208 N against 712 N).
- **The model:** it leaves the sand in place under a foot. With the wrench solver weakened so that the feet pass
  12 to 17 cm into the bed (`mass_e1.json`, `mass_e1all.json` above), 14 to 82 particles per environment are
  displaced by more than 2 cm, against about 2,200 in MPM with the feet 6.5 cm deep.
- **The coupling:** the wrench is computed analytically from the particles near the foot with an effective mass
  of 30 particle masses (the G1 example's setting), within a band of 8 cm around the collider and a 2 cm foot
  margin, so particles that do not move still carry the full weight, and the force starts before the sole reaches
  the surface. Lowering the scale lets the feet sink, but then the sand does not carry the robot (table above).
- **Not a setting:** stepping all 20,000 particles or switching sleeping off changes the foot height by about
  1 cm; the time step (5 ms), the particle spacing and the material are those of NeRD's training data; the patch
  is 1.6 m wide and the feet are in its middle.
- **What NeRD was trained on, in one line:** single rigid objects of 0.84 to 4.4 kg (box, cube, sphere, cylinder)
  pushed, pressed, dropped or resting, and falling sand, on a 2.0 x 1.2 x 0.3 m bed of one dry sand with a flat,
  ±10° or 6 cm wavy surface; no foot, no load near a robot's weight.
- Not done: a prescribed-motion test (the same foot trajectory imposed in both simulators). The comparison above
  is closed loop: the same policy and commands, but the motions differ once the feet meet the sand.

**Hardest terrain.** 20 environments, initial levels drawn from 0 to 9, 500 steps, MPM policy: NeRD finite, no sand
episode ended early (MPM, same sand: 0.2 per environment); foot origin +0.003 m (NeRD) against -0.072 m (MPM).
