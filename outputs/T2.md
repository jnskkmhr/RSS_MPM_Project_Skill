## Does the sand give way under the baselines' push-off? Reading fixed before computing (2026-11-25)

Asked for by the lead. Junnosuke's proposed explanation of why the retrained baselines stall with the cube on MPM sand: "On MPM, terrain is more slippery and penetrates, so it is hard to earn repulsion force enough to push object. I guess it is same as jumping hard on deformable terrain than concrete." Nothing below has been computed when this is written. No new runs: the existing rollouts are used.

**Data.** The two retrained baselines (`bc1o99un`, `5x48qh9t`) and `her588wk` with the 8 and 10 kg cubes, command 0.5 m/s, 16 trials per cell, on rigid ground (`logs/t2/tow_base2_home`) and on four MPM sands (dense sand, beach sand, packed poppy seed, loose poppy seed; `logs/t2/tow_base2`, `logs/t2/tow_sands_ctrl`); for item 3 the baselines with 15 kg on rigid ground. Logged at 50 Hz: foot poses, the ground's force on each foot (three components), pelvis and torso pose, tether force. Not recorded: the sand's particles, so the sand displaced under the sole cannot be given; the centre of mass is not logged, so the pelvis position stands in for it and that is said with the number.

**Definitions.**
- Loaded stance of a foot: its vertical ground force above 165 N (about half the body weight), from 5 s on, in trials that stay up; a stance needs at least 3 samples.
- Horizontal foot force: the component of the ground's force on the foot along the robot's direction of travel (propulsive when positive); mean and peak (largest 0.06 s mean) per stance.
- Slide: how far the sole centre moves backward along the direction of travel during the loaded stance. Sink: how far the sole centre moves down during the loaded stance.
- Push event: the tether tension (0.2 s mean) rises through the 90th percentile of its own trial. For the 0.3 s after it: slide and sink of the foot that is loaded at the event, and whether it stays loaded. For the 1.0 s after it against the 0.3 s before it: horizontal foot force (sum of both feet), torso pitch, pelvis forward speed.
- Effective traction: horizontal over vertical foot force, per stance (at the peak), and in the samples where the loaded foot moves back faster than 5 cm/s or down faster than 5 cm/s.
