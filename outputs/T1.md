## 6. Failed attempts: what was tried and did not work, and why.

Historical failures/limitations below are supported by local notes/results or conversation reports, not rerun now:

- Comparing GUI FPS across the two RTX paths was misleading: main presented DLSS intermediate frames while the experimental window selected the latest image. Earlier headless RTX comparisons also did not request an image each step and were invalid as interactive-rendering benchmarks.
- Enabling captured physics alone did not eliminate cached-patch latency spikes; the early eager/captured p95 values remained roughly 0.67–0.69 s/control step.
- Inactive reserve slots left near the patch center/world Z=0 entered active grid cells even when flagged inactive. The diagnostic attributes expensive FEM integration loops to position-based binning; parking reserve slots outside the active domain reduced spikes. The `cache_feet_only` diagnostic was worse (mean 356.48 ms/control step), so restricting to feet was not sufficient. These contaminated timings are not a controlled benchmark.
- Per-material template generation caused expensive randomized resets. Current main notes describe reusing one initial-material reference template instead; this trades initialization fidelity for eliminating repeated settling.
- Experimental two-second dune settling exposed escaping particles and plastic-volume overflow. Notes say nonfinite snapshots were rejected and one-second preparation passed. Longer relaxation was not demonstrated safe/equilibrated.
- Surface rendering could grow expensive with visited area and had background seams/visibility problems. Subsequent implementations restrict or cache meshes and filter particle display; these are rendering changes, not force-fidelity validation.
- Merely configuring a small compact working set is unsafe when startup/reset wakes the whole resident bed. The documented default retained full evaluation capacity; no proven large VRAM reduction follows from sleeping alone.
