## A. Scope agreed with Junnosuke (not yet recorded in `DECISIONS.md` or `tasks/T1.md`)

**T1 uses a primitive probe only: a box on a PD-driven gantry, with no robot and no policy.** T1 evaluates the terrain representation; robot results belong to later tasks. The probe gives prescribed trajectories, so force and sinkage can be compared across modes without gait divergence. Fx, Fy and Fz come from the coupled solver's per-body force buffer.

**Modes (same task family, material, voxel 0.04 m, sand depth 0.25 m, timestep; only domain management differs):**
- `full`: the patch spans the whole bed with its center pinned, so nothing is recycled. This is the reference.
- `recycle_nocache`: the 1.2 x 1.2 m patch follows the tracked body; terrain that re-enters is reset to its initial state. **Proposed primary method.**
- `recycle_cache`: the same patch plus the particle cache. Optional upgrade; see the cost evidence in section B.

**Results T1 will show**
1. Cost vs traversed distance (peak VRAM, step time mean/p95, physics time per step), all modes.
2. Cost vs number of environments: VRAM per env, time per step per env, largest env count that fits.
3. Fidelity: Fx, Fy, Fz time series and sinkage against `full`, for an indentation and a constant-speed drag.
4. Revisit: drag out and back along the same track; height-map and force error on the return pass for all three modes.
5. Cost of the cache: extra VRAM per env, and the limit on revisitable area (derived from 1 and 2).

Sinkage = depth of the body's lowest point below the undisturbed sand surface at the body position, positive downward. As implemented in the probe, the surface height is the median top-of-particle height in a 0.6 x 0.6 m window under the box after a 2 s pre-settle in the same run. The background mesh height is not used because the sand consolidates about 5-6 cm below it.

**Dropped from T1:** the policy-level cache comparison (acceptance criterion 5; needs a robot and training), and patch-size, boundary-width and discretization-step ablations.
**Deferred:** a recycling design space (zero state, settled template, statistical prior from offline settled states, cache); sleeping rows run in Max's stack as one controlled row. The experimental sleeping line stays out of the baselines, consistent with `DECISIONS.md` (2026-11-15).

## B2. Primitive probe: built, first results are preliminary

`scripts/benchmarks/t1/bench_probe.py` replaces the robot of the patch tasks by a 0.2 x 0.2 x 0.1 m, 10 kg box on a three-axis gantry (PD, stiffness 1e5 N/m, damping 4e3 N s/m), built only by overriding the task configuration (no shared code changed). The box is the only body handed to the particle solver in every mode. Trajectories: `rest`, `indent`, `drag`, `outback`. Force is the coupled solver's per-body force on the box (Fx, Fy, Fz, world frame, read once per control step). Depths are relative to the settled surface measured in the same run after a 2 s pre-settle. Sweeps in `run_sweeps.sh`: `probe_smoke`, `probe_terrain`, `probe_envs`, `probe_fidelity`.

**Preliminary result, RTX 5090 (not the 5080), 1 env, one run per mode, out-and-back with 3 m per pass, 0.5 m/s, drag depth 6 cm:**

Means are over the last 60% of each pass (skipping the entry transient); rms is over the whole pass, sample by sample against `full` (same trajectory, same time). Sinkage is the box underside depth below the settled surface measured in the same run.

Reading (preliminary): on fresh terrain `recycle_nocache` underestimates the mean plough force by about 19% and its recycled surface sits 1-2 cm above full-domain (loose zero-state sand). On the return pass its mean Fx is about 2.1x that of full-domain, because the trench is gone. The cache is closer to full-domain in the force and height rows but not exact: the return-pass mean Fx is about 25 N from full-domain, and the cache out-pass mean Fz differs by about 14 N. Indentation (`recycle_nocache`): force 350 N at 4 cm sinkage, 530 N at 7 cm, 730 N at 8 cm.

Things the probe showed that matter for the paper:
- The sand does not stay at the background mesh height. After placement it consolidates to about 5-6 cm below the nominal surface (median -0.054 m after 1 s, -0.061 m after 6 s), and the top surface is rough (5th-95th percentile spread about 6 cm). Height-map differences below about 3 cm are within this roughness, and a recycled patch needs a height offset to match the consolidated bed. This is the evidence for the proposed height offset and statistical initial state.
- `recycle_cache` starts from a settled template, so its settled surface is about 1 cm lower than the other two modes (-0.075 m vs -0.064 m).
- The no-cache force trace has repeated short spikes on the out pass that the other two modes lack. They may coincide with patch recentering (every 0.2 m of travel); this has not been checked against the recycle events.
- The no-cache height map is higher than full-domain along the patch edge the box moves toward, where recycled sand has just been placed at its initial height and not yet consolidated (an interpretation, consistent with the rest test above). On the back pass it has lost the trench.
- Videos of the out-and-back run for each mode (1920x1080 originals under `~/isaac/worktrees/IsaacLab-T1/logs/t1/video/`, one run per mode on the 5090, `bench_probe.py --video_dir`, Newton RTX visualizer) are on the slides at 1x speed. Only simulated particles are drawn (the cache clip also draws the saved, unloaded particles; the full-domain clip draws the whole bed).
- Repeated runs differ by about 0.4% in force (GPU non-determinism). The particle layout does not depend on the seed, so repeats do not sample different particle realizations.

## E. Caveats
- One trial per configuration and one seed; no confidence intervals yet.
- Section B uses a robot, which T1 will not; treat it as supplementary.
- In `full` mode, particles reaching the 0.2 m boundary ring become kinematic, a small difference from a free bed.
- **The `recycle_cache` vs `recycle_nocache` cost gap in section B is confounded.** Besides caching, the two G1 task configs differ in (1) which bodies are handed to the particle solver as colliders (nocache: only the ankle-roll links; cache: every rigid body in the env, `RIGID_PROXY_BODIES`), (2) CUDA-graph capture (nocache captures the whole physics step; cache keeps host paging outside the graph and captures only the solver, `use_cuda_graph=False`, `capture_solver=True`), and (3) the live particle slots (2.5x). The +9 ms step time and +320 MiB VRAM cannot be attributed to caching alone. The primitive probe removes (1) (one rigid body), but (2) and (3) are part of how the cache is built; they should be reported as separate factors, for example by also running `recycle_nocache` with the graph disabled.
- The probe sweeps (cost, fidelity, revisit) and the env-count sweep have not been run on the 5080. The probe numbers above come from the 5090, one run per mode, and must be repeated on the 5080 before use.
- The probe's vertical axis is position-controlled, so sinkage follows the commanded depth. A constant-load (force-controlled) press would make sinkage an outcome; it is not built.

## 6. Failed attempts: what was tried and did not work, and why.

Historical failures/limitations below are supported by local notes/results or conversation reports, not rerun now:

- Comparing GUI FPS across the two RTX paths was misleading: main presented DLSS intermediate frames while the experimental window selected the latest image. Earlier headless RTX comparisons also did not request an image each step and were invalid as interactive-rendering benchmarks.
- Enabling captured physics alone did not eliminate cached-patch latency spikes; the early eager/captured p95 values remained roughly 0.67–0.69 s/control step.
- Inactive reserve slots left near the patch center/world Z=0 entered active grid cells even when flagged inactive. The diagnostic attributes expensive FEM integration loops to position-based binning; parking reserve slots outside the active domain reduced spikes. The `cache_feet_only` diagnostic was worse (mean 356.48 ms/control step), so restricting to feet was not sufficient. These contaminated timings are not a controlled benchmark.
- Per-material template generation caused expensive randomized resets. Current main notes describe reusing one initial-material reference template instead; this trades initialization fidelity for eliminating repeated settling.
- Experimental two-second dune settling exposed escaping particles and plastic-volume overflow. Notes say nonfinite snapshots were rejected and one-second preparation passed. Longer relaxation was not demonstrated safe/equilibrated.
- Surface rendering could grow expensive with visited area and had background seams/visibility problems. Subsequent implementations restrict or cache meshes and filter particle display; these are rendering changes, not force-fidelity validation.
- Merely configuring a small compact working set is unsafe when startup/reset wakes the whole resident bed. The documented default retained full evaluation capacity; no proven large VRAM reduction follows from sleeping alone.

## 7. Known bugs and open issues.

- No matched cost/fidelity/cache benchmark satisfying T1 acceptance criteria has been located. Do not present the historical diagnostic tables as satisfying them.
- Task/dependency dirty trees must be preserved before a clean worktree can reproduce this state. No commit was made in this handoff step.
- Some old task imports and documentation paths are stale after the `velocity_mpm/` relocation. Experimental top-level documentation predates later paged-region work; prefer current task source and its README for feature ownership.
- Same checkpoint/seed is not identical robot motion. The eager/captured trajectory mismatch already demonstrates this confound. Matching force/sinkage requires prescribed trajectory replay or another explicitly controlled protocol.
- Cached unloaded material is frozen; it cannot reproduce ongoing remote avalanches, creep or stress redistribution. A settled flat reference template is approximate on slopes and after material randomization.
- Experimental finite-bed body regions do not bound resident particle memory. The generated paged adapter is documented as supporting one environment; multi-object region selection is not proof of multi-environment paged-adapter support.
- Default moving-region filtering retains moving/changing material outside tracked boxes; the support halo can extend beyond them. A hard cutoff changes the physical approximation. Small active particles near distant edges need not mean the bed is a tracking target.
- Grid activity, particle flags, support constraints, and cached material-history restoration must all remain valid across graph replay/reset. Existing tests cover subsets; full current integration correctness is not reverified.
- Main G1 asset collision filtering and Newton contact fixes can alter the reference. Current asset/backend versions must be held fixed in a new three-mode comparison; archived profile numbers use older states.
- Total host memory, GPU cache occupancy, mesh-pool growth, and paging/synchronization tails over long traveled distances remain unquantified here.
- A terrain-relative sinkage definition is still needed: measurement point on the foot, undeformed/reference surface height at that point, contact inclusion rule, and sign convention. Raw ankle height or an isosurface rendering is not automatically physical sinkage.
