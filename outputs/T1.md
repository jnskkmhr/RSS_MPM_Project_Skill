### Tile cache sizing (stream with cache)
The cache is sized per run instead of using one large allocation, because its allocation is part of the memory bound.
- **Tiles (`max_visited_tiles_per_env`):** the tiles the pool loads along the planned path, plus a one-tile border, because sand leaving the pool can land in the bordering tiles.
- **Records (`max_cached_particles_per_env`):** the sand of the loaded tiles that is outside the pool (891 particles per 0.2 m tile), plus the strips one update saves before it restores the entering strips (a row and a column of tiles, with 20% extra).
- **Slots (`simulation_capacity_multiplier`):** 1.10. Calibration runs with 2.0 peaked at 1.04 times the pool's particles.
- **Use against allocation:** slots 93%, records 90 to 99.5% (77% on the shortest corridor, where the strip reserve dominates), tiles 80 to 95%. The tables below list every run. No run hit a capacity.

### Sparse-grid reserve (P1 and P2)
The task reserves 8,192 sparse-grid leaf nodes per environment whatever sand an environment holds, about 290 MiB each. The first P1 and P2 runs used that default, and the streaming modes stopped at 32 environments on allocation failures. Junnosuke suspected the cell capacity; a test confirmed it (stream without cache at 8 environments: 701 to 413 MiB per environment).
- **Rule now used:** the probe sizes the reserve per run to the bounding box of the sand a mode simulates (the bed for `full`, the pool for the streaming modes), with one leaf of headroom in z, times 1.25. No task or solver code changed.
- **Effect:** streaming without cache and with cache (sleep off) now run 64 environments; `full` is unchanged, because its limit is its particles.
- **Both P1 and P2 below are from reruns with this rule** (2026-11-19, GPU otherwise idle). The runs with the default reserve are kept in `logs/t1/rtx5090/default_grid_reserve/`. The independent check in `checks/T1.md` was made on those earlier runs, not on the reruns.

### P1. Does it scale with terrain size? (step 2)
Corridor 1.6 m wide, one environment, box dragged `length_x` - 1.6 m at 1.0 m/s.

- **Stream without cache does not grow at all:** 1,094 MiB and 16.6 to 17.0 ms per step from 3.2 m to 102.4 m.
- **Stream with cache grows only by its saved sand:** 1,222 MiB and 17.0 ms at 3.2 m to 1,862 MiB and 19.3 ms at 102.4 m (3.6 M saved records).
- **Full grows with the terrain:** 1,286 MiB and 19.6 ms at 3.2 m to 16,410 MiB and 309 ms at 102.4 m (3.14 M particles). At 102.4 m it uses 15 times the memory and 18 times the step time of stream without cache.
- **Sleeping helps full as the terrain grows, and costs memory:** at 51.2 m the step is 86.5 ms with sleep on against 148.1 ms off; at 102.4 m, 164.9 ms against 309.1 ms, with 21,146 MiB against 16,410 MiB. At 3.2 m sleeping is slower (21.8 against 19.6 ms).
- **Sleeping does not help the streaming modes here:** it adds 1.0 to 1.5 ms per step without the cache and 0.1 to 1.8 ms with it, and 66 to 98 MiB. The region kept awake (1.2 x 1.2 m) is as large as the pool's simulated window. Without the cache no simulated particle was asleep at the end of any run; with the cache up to 1,637 of about 33,000 were.
- **GPU utilization** was 93 to 100% in every run, so no mode was waiting on the host.

### P2. Does it scale with the number of environments? (step 3)
Each environment has 8 x 8 m of terrain; the box travels 6.4 m at 1.0 m/s.

- **Largest count that ran on the 32 GB card:** full 4 (sleep off) or 2 (sleep on); stream without cache 64 (sleep off and on); stream with cache 64 (sleep off) or 32 (sleep on). Counts were doubled, so the true limit lies between that count and the next. All failures at the next count are allocation failures.
- **Memory per environment at the largest count:** full 6,321 MiB; stream without cache 327 MiB; stream with cache 451 MiB.
- **Step time per environment at the largest count:** full 126 ms; stream without cache 5.2 ms; stream with cache 5.8 ms.
- **Sleeping on full:** it shortens the step (77.4 against 112.8 ms at one environment) but needs about 25% more memory, which halves the count that fits.
- **Sleeping on the streaming modes:** the step per environment changes by at most 1.4 ms (at one environment) and by about 0.2 ms or less from 8 environments up, in either direction, while memory rises 8 to 19% (17 to 19% at 32 and 64 environments; stream without cache at 64: 24,924 against 20,924 MiB). With the cache, 64 environments fit with sleep off (28,882 MiB) and fail with sleep on, which is consistent with that extra memory; the failing run's peak was not measured.

## Training-speed benchmark (2026-11-20 to 2026-11-22, in progress, unchecked)

**Not part of the T1 task file.** Junnosuke's mentor asked for policy convergence speed in wall-clock time: streaming MPM against full MPM, same GPUs. Junnosuke asked this session to build and run it. It needs a decision by the lead on where it belongs (a new task, or an addition to T1 or T2). Nothing here is checked, and no training run has finished.

### Design, as agreed with Junnosuke
- **Plot:** wall-clock time on x; tracking error, fall rate and reward on y. Both runs on identical hardware, each with the largest environment count that fits on a GPU.
- **Start from a pretrained policy, not from scratch.** Both runs finetune the MJWarp rigid rough-terrain policy `wandb:jnskkmr/g1_29dof_contact_benchmark/3qymp37c` (iteration 19,999). The metric is therefore time to adapt to sand, not time to learn to walk.
- **Pure MPM**, no rigid environments.
- **Terrain:** one 8 x 8 m inverted-pyramid tile at a fixed slope of 0.4 (rise over run, about 22 degrees) with a 2 m platform at the centre; every environment is on it, each in its own sand. No terrain curriculum.
- **No curriculum of any kind;** standard projected gravity with uniform noise 0.05; command ranges vx (-1.0, 1.5), vy (-0.5, 0.5), yaw rate (-1.0, 1.0) m/s and rad/s; no video.
- **Sand:** depth 0.25 m, voxel 0.04 m, `poppy_seed_loose`, the same in both tasks. Material randomization at reset is on (the `g1_29dof` events).
- **Streaming:** tile cache, sleep off. **Full:** the whole 8 x 8 m bed, fixed grid, sleep off.

### Code (unified IsaacLab, `jnskkmhr/mpm-unification`, all uncommitted)
- **Tasks:** `source/isaaclab_tasks/isaaclab_tasks/contrib/velocity_mpm/speed_bench/` with `mdp/`, `util/`, `config/mpm/{agents, env_cfg, streaming_mpm_env.py, streaming_mpm_env_cfg.py, mpm_env.py, mpm_env_cfg.py}`. IDs `IsaacContrib-Velocity-G1-29dof-MPM-SpeedBench-Stream` and `-Full`. It is a standalone copy of `velocity_mpm/config/g1_29dof` as of 2026-11-21; the env files and the MDP terms are unchanged.
- **Differences from `g1_29dof`:** the slope tile; curricula removed; standard projected gravity; video recorders removed; the full bed is 8 x 8 m of the streaming task's sand under the same tile; cache and sparse-grid reserves sized to the tile (cache slots 2.0, the `g1_29dof` value); streaming defaults to cache with sleep off and full to the fixed grid.
- **Full shares the streaming task's terrain.** The full beds are not spread on a grid: every bed lies under the one shared terrain tile, each in its own simulation world, and the task reuses the streaming terrain importer and height scanner. With a per-environment scanner the rays hit the robot's own body and the terrain-aware rewards were 10 to 40 times off; with the shared tile both tasks read identical heights and rewards at the first step (root 1.007 m above the scanned ground, base-height reward -0.6671, foot clearance 4.4712).

**What differs besides terrain management:**
- **MPM grid type:** sparse for streaming (the pool moves, and the cache requires it), fixed for full (its faster type; see "Grid type of `full`").
- **Sand boundary:** the streaming pool is bounded by its ring of kinematic particles; the full bed by a support mesh and four retaining walls at the tile edge.
- **Fresh sand:** streaming tiles come from the cache's settled tile template; the full bed starts from sampled particles and is settled for 1 s at creation, with that snapshot restored at reset.
- **Reset:** streaming resets an environment's pool and its cache; full restores the whole bed's snapshot.
- **Environment class:** `G1MPMEnv` against `G1TestbedEnv` (a subclass that adds the bed snapshot and the rigid support under the bed).
- **Out-of-bounds termination:** different functions with the same limit, 0.7 m inside the tile edge.
- **PPO batch:** follows from the environment count; the PPO settings are identical.
- **Identical:** robot, actions, observations, rewards, events (including reset-time material randomization), commands, terrain tile, height scanner, sand depth, voxel and material, timestep, starting checkpoint, seed.

**Metric against wall-clock time (W&B `_runtime`):** `Metrics/base_velocity/error_vel_xy` and `error_vel_yaw`, and the fraction of episodes ending in a fall (`Episode_Termination/bad_orientation`); mean reward and episode length as secondary curves; total samples as a second x axis.

- **No cache is 15 to 17% faster than cache** and uses about half the memory. An earlier local measurement that showed the opposite was an artifact: the copied `g1_29dof` config records video for the first 200 steps of every run, and drawing the sand costs more without the cache.
- **Against the T2 tow job** (`her588wk`, 8.6 s per iteration with 32 environments per L40, half of them rigid, 11.2 ms per environment step): this task at 32 environments, all on sand, takes 5.0 s per iteration and 6.5 ms per environment step.
- **Resets are not a bottleneck** with Newton `e681453d` (which contains `01d6e3d3`): in a 32-environment profile the reset itself is 1.2% of the environment step and the solver re-reading the model after reset-time randomization 8.8%; physics is 75.5%.
- The flat (L40) and slope (L40S) full rows differ in how the beds are placed (spread out against all under one tile), so their memory is not directly comparable.

### Findings that affect other tasks
- **Reset-time material randomization was the dominant cost before Newton `01d6e3d3`** (T3's fix of the coupled attribute projection; `e681453d` contains it). Each change made the coupled solver re-read attributes for all particles on the host: 32 environments took 35 to 45 s per iteration with it and 5 s without (67 of 87 s of stepping in a profile). On OSMO, 64 environments from scratch took 130 to 180 s per iteration and exceeded 48 GB of host memory. Any `g1_29dof` training on a Newton older than `01d6e3d3` pays this. T3 found the same code path leaking host memory (`outputs/T3.md`, "Notes for T1 from the mixed-terrain training").
- **`g1_29dof`'s default cache and grid reserves do not fit many environments on a small tile:** the streaming task ran out of GPU memory at 32 environments on a 32 GB RTX 5090 with the defaults.
- **Cache slots on a slope:** 1.10 slots per pool particle (enough on flat ground in T1, peak 1.04) ran out after 14 iterations on the slope, where sand slides downhill and piles up. The benchmark now uses 2.0.
- **Training from scratch is too slow to plot.** Two from-scratch runs (flat, voxel 0.03 m, no curricula, 16 L40) were stopped after 24.8 h: streaming at iteration 4,396 with episode length 101 of 1,000, full at 3,120 with 60. Per sample, streaming tracked the reference run `eayrj79r` (40 x 64 environments, about 5,800 samples per second, walking after 12 to 24 h); it collected 1,262 samples per second and full 69.
- **Flat sand gives no convergence signal for a pretrained policy.** In the first 75 minutes of two finetuning runs on flat sand, 0 to 3% of episodes ended in a fall and the linear tracking error stayed at 0.2 to 0.35 m/s without a trend. Hence the slope tile.

### Note for the lead on the Newton fixes and the cache's training-time cost (2026-11-22)
- **Which Newton commit T1's results are tied to.** P1, P2, P3, step 5 and the grid-type comparison ran on Newton `6fee8b0b` plus the then-uncommitted streaming, cache and sleep files, which are commit `7adb5be7` unchanged. None ran on `01d6e3d3` or `e681453d`.
- **Do the two fixes affect a T1 number?** Not expected, and not rerun to confirm. `01d6e3d3` changes the coupler's projection after a particle material change; the T1 probe never changes materials and never resets an environment. `e681453d` lets the cache skip worlds without particles; every world in T1 holds sand.
- **Where they do matter:** the training-speed benchmark. Its first two sizing tests (`speed-bench-test-1`, `-2`) and the from-scratch and flat finetuning runs used `7adb5be7`; everything from `speed-bench-test-3` on, including the slope runs, uses `e681453d`. The table in "Measured" is all on `e681453d`.
- **The cache's cost in training against P2.** P2 (box probe, voxel 0.03 m, RTX 5090, no resets) has the cache 12% slower than no cache at 64 environments (5.83 against 5.16 ms per environment step). In training (G1, voxel 0.04 m, L40 and L40S, with resets) it is 19 to 20% slower at 32 and 64 environments (6.5 against 5.4 ms; 7.4 to 7.5 against 6.2 ms). T3's mixed task shows 24% (17.4 against 14.0 s per iteration). So the three agree in direction and the cost is larger in training than in the box probe. Why it is larger was not measured; the robot covers ground faster than the 1 m/s box path per step of sand it disturbs, and every reset reloads an environment's tiles, both of which add paging.

## Making the warm-start reset per environment: proposal, the scaling result, and a prediction (2026-11-26, nothing run, unchecked)

Asked by the lead after T3 found (unchecked) that a walking policy's outcome depends on how many robots share a run (0 walking goals of 40 with 5 or 4 robots, 21 with 2; pelvis 9.4 mm higher with 2 than with 4). From reading Newton `5ececcbe`; line numbers approximate. Nothing here was applied or run.

**The solver already has a per-environment version of everything the streaming pool resets globally. The global reset comes from one scalar flag in the pool and from two calls made without a mask.**

### 1. Proposal for Junnosuke (not applied): reset per environment

What is global today, and the per-environment form that exists:

| Step | Today | Per-environment form already in the code |
|---|---|---|
| Deciding that something changed | one scalar `changed` for the run (`finalize_update` in `particle_cache_kernels.py`, line 600, from run-wide counters); without the cache one scalar `mass_change_count` (`_recycle_particles` in `particle_streaming.py`) | none, but every kernel that feeds the counters already knows the particle's world (`world = worlds[i]` in `plan_transitions`; `particle_world[i]` in `_recycle_particles`; the fresh, restore and reset kernels carry the world index) |
| Clearing the contact warm start | `_clear_reset_warmstarts(None, (None, None))` (`particle_streaming.py`, lines 427 and 672) | `_clear_reset_warmstarts(world_mask, reset_partitions)` (`solver_implicit_mpm.py`, line 3033): with a mask it clears only the masked worlds' stored impulses (`reset_values_by_lattice_key`), point warm starts (`reset_mpm_point_warmstart`) and grid warm starts (`reset_mpm_grid_warmstart`). This is the path the solver's own masked `reset(world_mask)` uses (line 3170) |
| Resetting the collider history | `save_collider_current_position(body_q, body_world, world_count)` without a mask | the same function takes `world_mask` (line 1042) and then updates only the masked worlds' bodies |

The smallest change, in three places:
1. **A per-world change mask in the pools.** One boolean array of `world_count`, zeroed at the start of each update and set to true for the particle's world wherever a counter is incremented today: in `plan_transitions` (outgoing particles and layer changes), in the fresh-tile, restore and reset kernels, and in `_recycle_particles` for the pool without cache. The scalar flag stays, because `wp.capture_if` needs one condition to decide whether to run the refresh at all.
2. **Pass that mask** in both `_refresh_particle_mass` functions: `self.solver._clear_reset_warmstarts(mask, partitions)` and `save_collider_current_position(..., world_mask=mask, ...)`.
3. **The partitions argument.** With a mask, grid-backed warm-start fields need their space partitions, which the masked `reset` gets from `_validate_reset_warmstart_fields` on the host; that call is not allowed inside a captured graph. In the task's configuration it is not needed: the contact impulses are stored by lattice key and the collider basis is particle-based (`pic27`), both of which the masked path handles without a partition. So the proposal is to pass `(None, None)` with the mask and to refuse, at configuration time, a pool combined with grid-backed warm-start fields. I did not check every configuration the solver accepts.

What else would have to become per environment:
- **Nothing more for correctness of the reset.** `refresh_cached_properties` rewrites derived particle properties for all particles from the model, which is harmless; `settle_particles` already acts on the arrived particles only; `refresh_material` restarts the sleeping planner's countdown for the whole run, which makes the next planning pass come earlier and changes no state that I can see (not traced further).
- **The iteration count: it can stay shared.** An environment that has converged goes on iterating while another has not; extra Gauss-Seidel sweeps on a converged environment only refine it. Letting converged environments stop early would save time and would need the solve loop to skip environments, which is a larger change (the loop is one captured `while` over all environments). It is not needed for independence in the strict sense either way: with the cap of 25 binding, every environment gets 25 sweeps whatever the others do; without it binding, an environment gets at least as many sweeps as it would alone.
- **What the change would not remove:** an environment's own window changes still clear its own warm start (every 0.2 m of travel), and the difference between the one-environment code path and the multi-environment one.
- **Is clearing needed at all?** Not examined. The sand that changes state at a window step is at the window's edge, 0.6 m from the robot, while the contact impulses that matter are under the feet; clearing only the impulses near the changed tiles would be a further step. That is a solver design question for its authors.
- **Tests it would need:** two environments, one idle and one crossing tiles, with the idle one's contact impulses compared before and after the other's window step; and the existing masked-reset tests rerun.

### 2. The merged scaling result (cost against the number of environments)

- **What was measured stands:** memory and step time per environment with every environment doing the same drag.
- **Does any merged statement imply that environments are independent?** Not in words. The statements are about how many environments fit and what each costs. But "per environment" numbers are easily read as "each environment is the same simulation as one alone", and that is not what was tested: the one-environment rows use another code path, and with two or more the environments share the iteration count and the moments at which contact warm starts are discarded.
- **What T1 did test since:** the drag on a box in one environment is the same with 1, 2, 8 and 32 environments, idle, crossing tiles or dragging (within 5.6 N of repeat spread), up to warm starts being discarded in 20.5% of the steps; the vertical force on the box fell from about 10 to about 5 N at that rate. T3's walking result says a robot is affected.
- **Sentence to put beside the scaling result:** "The environments of one run do not exchange sand, but they share the solver's iteration count and they all lose their contact warm start whenever any one of them changes its window. The cost per environment was measured with all environments doing the same drag. A box's drag in one environment did not depend on the others in a later test (unchecked); a walking robot's outcome did in T3's evaluation (unchecked)."

### 3. Prediction for the trainings (to be tested, not a result)

- **How often, by arithmetic:** with 32 sand environments per process and robots at about 0.7 m/s, at least one window steps in x in about 43% of the 5 ms physics steps; y steps, particles drifting across window edges and resets add to it. With 2 robots it is about 3.5%, with 5 about 8.4%, from tile steps alone. So in training the contact warm start would be cleared in most steps or close to it. For T3's evaluations the tile steps alone give under a tenth of the steps; how much sand drifting across the window edges adds on a slope is not known, so the true rate there may be much higher.
- **What that predicts:** each physics step in which the warm start was cleared starts the contact solve from zero impulse and has 25 sweeps to build up the support under a foot. If 25 sweeps are too few, the sand a training robot feels gives less support than the sand a single robot feels: the foot sinks further before the force builds, and the support is noisier from step to step. The direction agrees with what was seen: T3's pelvis rides lower with 4 robots than with 2, and my box's vertical force halved at a 20% clearing rate while its drag did not change.
- **What it would mean if true:** a policy trained with 32 sand environments per process learned on softer sand than the sand it is evaluated on with 2 to 5 robots, and results would depend on the number of environments per process.
- **How to test it:** T3's cap test (5 robots with the cap at 100 and 400) separates "too few sweeps after a cleared warm start" from other causes: if the result with 5 robots moves to the result with 2 when the cap is raised, the prediction holds. A direct test: one robot standing or walking in environment 0 while the other environments' windows are stepped at a set rate, recording foot sinkage against that rate.
- **Not known:** whether 25 sweeps are too few for a foot; whether the stress warm start, which I do not see cleared, limits the effect; whether sleeping changes it (T3's counts are with sleeping).

## Do the environments of one run influence each other? Code reading and plan (2026-11-26, plan not run, unchecked)

Asked by the lead to verify three statements from the T3 session's code reading. Newton `5ececcbe`, IsaacLab `819ff3622`; line numbers approximate.

**All three hold in the code, with one narrowing: what is cleared for every environment is the contact warm start; in the task's configuration I do not find a stress warm start that is cleared. Sand is never exchanged between environments. What they share is the number of solver iterations and the moments at which the contact warm start is thrown away.**

### The three statements

1. **With one environment, `separate_worlds` is switched off: yes.** `self._separate_worlds = bool(config.separate_worlds and self._initial_world_count > 1)` (`solver_implicit_mpm.py`, line 1972). With one environment the solver builds one grid from all particles, has no per-environment offsets, and uses the "shared" variants of its kernels and the scalar stopping rule; with two or more it uses per-environment grids, the "isolated" kernel variants and the batched stopping rule. For a single environment the two stopping rules test the same quantity (the tolerance is scaled by the size of the system, which is then the one environment), so the mathematics is the same. The code path is not, and the solve stops at 25 iterations before converging (T8), so I cannot say from the code that the numbers are identical.
2. **One iteration count for all environments: yes.** The stress and contact loop is one `capture_while` over a shared counter; `update_batched_condition` (`solve_rheology.py`, line 268) stops it when every environment's residual is under its tolerance or the count passes `max_iterations` (25). An environment that has converged goes on iterating as long as any other has not.
3. **A change in any environment's window clears warm starts in every environment: yes for the contact warm start.**
   - With the cache: `finalize_update` (`particle_cache_kernels.py`, line 600) sets one flag `changed` for the whole run if any particle of any environment left, changed layer, was generated, restored or reset. `CachedParticleStreaming.update` then calls `_refresh_particle_mass` (`particle_streaming.py`, line 632), which ends with `self.solver._clear_reset_warmstarts(None, (None, None))` and `save_collider_current_position(...)` without a mask: all environments.
   - Without the cache the same call is made when any particle of any environment changes between ring and window (`particle_streaming.py`, lines 345 and 427). So this is not specific to the cache.
   - What is cleared: the stored contact impulses (`impulse_values`, `ws_impulse_field`). A grid-backed stress warm start (`ws_stress_field`) would be cleared too, but with `warmstart_mode = "auto"` on the task's sparse grid the stress warm start is carried on the particles (`Config.warmstart_mode` docstring) and I do not see it cleared here. Not checked by running.
   - The collider history that is reset (`body_q_prev`) is read for collider velocities only in the `"backward"` mode (line 3620); the task uses `"forward"`. It is also passed to the sleeping planner; what a reset does there I did not trace.
   - A layer change is counted for a live particle that drifts across the window's edge, not only when the window steps by a tile, so the flag can fire more often than tile crossings.

### What it means for a quantity measured in one environment

- **Force on the box in environment 0:** not through the sand (grids, particles and colliders are per environment). Possibly through 2 and 3: if the other environments keep the loop running, environment 0 gets more iterations than it would alone; if any other environment changes its window, environment 0 starts its contact solve from zero instead of from the last step. Both act on a solve that is cut off at 25 iterations, so they can change the force. By how much is not known from the code.
- **If the cap of 25 always binds** (T8 found the standard runs stop at the cap), statement 2 has no effect on forces or on step time: every environment gets 25 iterations whatever the others do. It matters only where some environment would have stopped early, for example idle sand.
- **Step time per environment:** every environment pays for the iterations of the slowest one. In T1's environment sweep all environments ran the same drag, so this did not vary; the sweep's one-environment rows ran the shared code path and its rows with two or more the isolated one, so the step from 1 to 2 environments mixes the two.
- **How often 3 fires in a walking training with 32 sand environments per GPU (arithmetic, not measured):** a robot at 0.7 m/s crosses a 0.2 m tile boundary in x on 1.75% of the 5 ms steps. With 32 independent robots at least one crosses in about 43% of the steps, from x alone; y crossings, particles drifting across window edges and resets add to it. So in training the contact warm start is discarded in roughly half of the steps or more, where a one-environment probe at 0.5 m/s discards it in about 1.25% of the steps.
- **Are T1's one-environment probes the same solver as the multi-environment task:** the same equations, a different code path (statement 1), and a very different rate of discarded contact warm starts (statement 3). Whether the numbers agree is what the test below measures.

## Where the streaming grids are anchored, and what an off-grid environment origin changes (2026-11-26, unchecked)

Asked by the lead after T3's finding (block `T3-grid-for-T1` in `outputs/T3.md`) that a walking result moves with the lane's origin. From reading the code in `~/isaac/worktrees/mpm-unification/newton` (Newton `5ececcbe`), no runs. Line numbers are approximate.

**Summary.** With the tile cache on, the window of live sand is first snapped on a grid anchored at the environment's origin and then snapped again onto the world's tile grid, and the second position is the one used. An origin that is not a multiple of 0.2 m therefore shifts the live window against the robot: by -0.013 m in T3's three weak cells and by +0.087 m in its two strong cells. The same origins also differ by half a voxel in where the terrain and the robot sit on the MPM grid, so T3's two offsets cannot tell the two effects apart.

### 1. Anchors

| Thing | Anchored at | Where in the code |
|---|---|---|
| MPM grid (cells of 0.04 m) | world origin, multiples of the voxel | sparse grid built from particle voxels, `_allocate_grid` in `solver_implicit_mpm.py`; fixed and dense grids round the particles' bounding box to voxel multiples (about lines 3980 to 3990) |
| Live window, no cache | environment origin + whole tiles | `_update_window_center` in `particle_streaming.py` (line 32): `origin + floor((x - origin) / tile + 0.5) * tile` |
| Live window, cache on | world origin + whole tiles | `update_patch_center` in `particle_cache_kernels.py` (line 23) does the same environment-anchored snap, then `compute_tile_begins` (line 319) overwrites the centre with `(begin + shape / 2) * tile`, where `begin = rint(centre / tile - shape / 2)`. `plan_transitions` (line 96) uses that overwritten centre to decide which particles are live |
| Tile index of the cache | world origin | `tile = floor(x / tile_size)` in `plan_transitions` (line 111) |
| Sand lattice, no cache | the environment's first pool position | `template_positions` are the spawned particle positions, wrapped by the pool size (`_recycle_particles`, line 92) |
| Sand lattice, cache on | world origin, mirrored on odd tiles | fresh tiles are placed at `tile index * tile_size + local position`, mirrored when the index is odd (kernel at line 561) |
| Terrain surface, robot, lane | environment origin | scene |

### Does an off-grid origin change what the solver computes

- **Cache on, origin not a multiple of the tile (0.2 m): yes, in two ways.**
  - *The live window moves against the robot.* With `shape = 8` tiles (1.6 m: the `g1_29dof` task's 1.2 m window plus a 0.2 m ring on each side; I did not check that T3's evaluation scene uses the same pool, and an odd number of tiles would change the shifts by half a tile), the used centre is the environment-anchored centre moved to the nearest world grid point. For an origin that is `r` above a multiple of 0.2 m the shift is `-r` if `r < 0.1` and `0.2 - r` if `r > 0.1`. T3's lanes: origin -3.187 and -4.787 m give `r = 0.013`, shift -0.013 m; origin -4.687 and -1.687 m give `r = 0.113`, shift +0.087 m. The pelvis is then between -0.087 and +0.113 m from the window centre in the first case and between -0.187 and +0.013 m in the second, in x. In the second case the robot has about 10 cm more live sand on the +x side and 10 cm less on the -x side before the ring of fixed particles. Whether +x is the uphill direction in T3's lanes I did not check.
  - *The sand lattice moves against the lane.* Tile seams and the mirrored tiles sit 0.1 m further along the lane in one case than in the other.
- **Cache on or off, origin not a multiple of the voxel (0.04 m): yes.** The terrain surface, the robot and (without the cache) the sand lattice shift against the MPM grid. T3's origins are 0.013 m and 0.033 m above a voxel multiple, a difference of half a voxel. **(Corrected below: T3's evaluation voxel is 0.02 m, for which both origin groups have the same phase.)** T8 measured that shifting a bed against the grid changes the box force by up to 19% at this voxel size.
- **No cache, origin not a multiple of the tile: no effect from the tile.** The window and the sand lattice are both anchored at the environment, so the lane frame sees the same thing; only the voxel effect above remains.
- **Confirmed later by the T3 session (its message of 2026-11-26, not checked by me):** T3's walking scene uses the same pool (1.2 m window plus 0.2 m ring, 8 tiles), and +x is uphill in its planar +15 deg cells, so the strong cells have the extra 10 cm of live sand on the uphill side. T3 also measured the shift in two short local runs with three robots: window centre minus pelvis differs by 0.074 to 0.099 m between the two lane placements, against 0.10 m from the formula above.
- **Not established:** which of the two (window shift or voxel phase) produces T3's result, and that either does. At `r` exactly 0.1 m the second snap is a rounding tie and may go either way.

### What the correct behaviour would be (proposal for Junnosuke, not applied)

The live window should be centred on the tracked body to within half a tile whatever the environment's origin. With the cache the window also has to sit on whole tiles, because sand is saved and restored tile by tile, so the window grid and the tile grid must be the same grid.

- **Which snap should win:** the world-anchored one, which is already the one in effect. The fault is that the first snap rounds the body's position on a different grid, so the second snap moves the result by up to half a tile instead of leaving it alone.
- **Corrected after the check (see "Corrections after the check of 2026-11-26"): the change below is right only for an even number of tiles per side, and the no-cache kernel should be left as it is.**
- **Smaller change: anchor the window grid to the world.** In `update_patch_center` (`particle_cache_kernels.py`, lines 41 and 42) snap `floor(p / step + 0.5) * step` without subtracting the environment origin. `compute_tile_begins` then finds the centre already on its grid and does not move it. Two lines, one kernel. For the same behaviour without the cache, the same two lines in `_update_window_center` (`particle_streaming.py`, lines 47 and 48); that mode works at any centre because its sand wraps around the pool.
- **Larger change: anchor the tile index to the environment.** Every place that turns a position into a tile index would need the origin: `plan_transitions`, the fresh-tile placement and its mirror parity, `compute_tile_begins`, the tile bounds and the host mirrors in `particle_cache.py`. It would also make the sand lattice move with the origin against the MPM grid.
- **Without any code change:** keep environment origins on multiples of 0.2 m. T3's lanes are centred on the world, which puts their origins off the grid.
- **What neither change removes:** the position of the terrain and the robot against the 0.04 m MPM grid, and of the tile seams against the lane, still depend on the origin unless origins are multiples of the tile.
- Not tested: either change.

### Correction, the r = 0 case of the proposal, and a larger-window control (2026-11-26, nothing run)

**Correction to the anchoring section above.** I wrote that T3's two origins differ by half a voxel in where the terrain and robot sit on the MPM grid. That used the 0.04 m voxel of the training task. T3's evaluation uses a 0.02 m voxel, and both of its origin groups are 0.015 m above a multiple of 0.02 m, so the voxel phase is the same in both groups and cannot explain T3's result. The half-voxel remark holds only for a 0.04 m voxel. (Found in Check 15 of `checks/T3.md`.)

**What the proposed two-line change does at r = 0.** Nothing, in exact arithmetic: with the origin a whole number of tiles, `origin + floor((x - origin) / tile + 0.5) * tile` and `floor(x / tile + 0.5) * tile` are the same number. In 32-bit floating point the two forms can round differently when the body is within about a micrometre of a half-tile boundary, so a window step could come one physics step earlier or later at such an instant; results would be the same in kind but not guaranteed bit-identical. The running trainings are not affected in any case, because the change is not applied and their origins have r = 0 (by the arithmetic of the 8 m terrain grid; I did not read origins from a training run).

From T1's box benchmark at 64 environments (451 MiB and 5.8 ms per environment with the cache) that is about 700 MiB and about 9 to 10 ms per environment. For T3's evaluation scene (0.02 m voxel, 0.50 m bed, 346,112 live particles per robot) the pool goes to about 541,000 particles per robot. Memory should follow the pool; step time should follow the live particles; neither scaling was tested.
- *Cheaper first step:* the control only needs evaluation runs (T3's cells, 40 trials each), not a retraining. A retraining with the larger window is a separate question (whether policies trained in a 1.2 m window carry an imprint of it).
- *The limit of the control:* full-domain MPM on the same lane is the window of infinite size and would be the cleanest reference, at the cost T1 measured for full.

## The box force is read at one moment of a 50 Hz cycle (2026-11-24, unchecked)

Asked by the lead after T8's finding that the MPM drag depends on the gantry stiffness. Small local tests on one RTX 5090 with the T1 probe, one run each, the force and box velocity recorded after every physics step (5 ms) and not only every control step (20 ms). Files: `~/isaac/worktrees/mpm-unification/IsaacLab/logs/t1/settle_diag_20261124/` (`substep_probe.py`, `drag_*`, `sub_*`, `hist_*`, `an_sub2.py`, `an_sub3.py`, `an_hist.py`).

**Summary.** The gantry's target position is updated once per control step, so the box speeds up and slows down 50 times a second, and the sand force follows. ### 3. Candidate cause, from the solver side

- **The speed cycle itself** is not a solver effect: the PD target is held for four physics steps and then jumps by speed x 20 ms, and a gantry whose lag time (damping / stiffness = 40 ms) is comparable to the control step follows it as a sawtooth. A stiffer gantry at the same damping ratio follows more sharply (speed 0.79 to 1.17 m/s) than the T1 gantry (0.91 to 1.04 m/s), because the sand force slows the softer one. A long lag time removes it.
- **Why the force follows the speed so strongly (from the code, not isolated by a test):** in the proxy coupling the sand solver sees the box as a free body with the mass MuJoCo reports for it (`body_invweight0`, about 10 kg here; it does not include the gantry's spring or damper), takes its velocity at the end of the rigid-body step, and returns `mass x velocity change / dt` as the force for the next step (`harvest_proxy_momentum_forces_kernel`, `mode = "lagged"`). When the box has just been accelerated, the sand in front of it has to be brought up to that speed within one 5 ms step, which costs a large impulse; when the box slows, the sand ahead is already moving and gives almost none. So within a cycle the force is dominated by accelerating and releasing the sand in front of the box, not by its steady resistance.
- **Not supported by these runs:** vertical micro-motion. The box height varies by 0.05 mm or less within the window on every gantry.

## Why the settled bed keeps sinking, and what it means for sleeping (2026-11-24, unchecked)

Asked by the lead after the T3 report ("what makes the sand stop yielding") and T8 (DEM against MPM). Answers come from reading the solver code and from two small local tests on one RTX 5090; no OSMO run. Files: `~/isaac/worktrees/mpm-unification/IsaacLab/logs/t1/settle_diag_20261124/`.

**Summary.** The bed sinks because the task samples about 2 particles per grid cell, which leaves 12 to 14% of the cells inside the bed empty, and the solver has no sand in an empty cell to resist the sand around it moving in. The sleep threshold (1 cm/s) is above the speed of this sinking, so sand goes to sleep while it is still compacting.

### a. Should a settled bed lose height, and what causes it

No. The material is modelled as incompressible sand (`critical_fraction = 0`, no hardening, no dilatancy), so a settled bed should stay at the density it is generated with.

- **Cause: too few particles per cell.** The task spawns 1.25 particles per cell edge, 1.95 per cell, with a 5 cm jitter (`streaming_scene_cfg.py`, `scene_cfg.py`). The sparse grid is built only from the voxels that contain a particle (`_allocate_grid` in `solver_implicit_mpm.py`, about line 3848), and with `critical_fraction = 0` the solver skips the fill-based constraint entirely (about line 5027). So a cell without a particle is simply not part of the sand, and the sand next to it can move into it.
- **The numbers agree with T8.** Sampling the spawner's lattice and jitter (`empty_cells.py`) gives the fraction of empty cells, and filling them gives a predicted density of 640 / (1 - empty):

  | Particles per cell edge | Voxel [cm] | Empty cells at spawn | Predicted density [kg/m3] | T8 measured [kg/m3] |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1.25 | 4 | 11.5% | 723 | 696 |
  | 1.25 | 3 | 12.4% | 731 | 731 |
  | 1.25 | 2 | 13.7% | 742 | 753 |
  | 1.25 | 1.5 | 13.8% | 743 | 758 |
  | 1.25 | 1 | 14.2% | 746 | 775 |
  | 1.6 | 4 / 2 | 1.0% / 1.6% | 646 / 650 | 652 / 651 |
  | 2 | 4 to 1.5 | 0% | 640 | 641 to 647 |

- **Direct test** (`occupancy_probe.py`, full bed 3.2 x 1.6 x 0.25 m, voxel 4 cm, sleep off, box hovering, one run): over 24 s the surface went from 3.0 to 4.1 cm below the generated level, the particles per cell inside the bed from 2.27 to 2.32 (1.95 at spawn), and the empty cells from 6.6% to 5.0%. The sinking slows (0.3 cm in the first second after the 1 s creation settle, under 0.01 cm/s at the end) but had not stopped. The cell count uses a 4 cm lattice that is not aligned with the solver's grid, so the percentages are indicative.
- **Why it never quite stops (my reading, not tested):** nothing keeps particles evenly spread inside a full cell, so as the sand moves, cells empty again and are refilled. The same holes lower the shear strength, which fits T8's creep from 20 deg and sliding at 32.5 deg instead of 36.1 deg; T8 reports no creep with 2 or 3 particles per edge.
- **Not the cause, by T8's one-at-a-time runs:** the iteration cap and tolerance (-1% / +4% in force, density unchanged within 1%) and the time step (+8% / +5%).
- **A setting under which the bed stays put:** 1.6 or more particles per cell edge (T8: density stays near 640 kg/m3; no creep at 2). Cost: 2.1 times the particles at 1.6 per edge, 4.1 times at 2. Not tested by me: whether the GIMP integration scheme (`integration_scheme = "gimp"`, which spreads each particle over the cells it overlaps) removes the empty cells at the current particle count.

### b. Are the sleep thresholds right

- **Thresholds in use:** particle speed below 0.01 m/s and velocity gradient below 0.25 1/s for 0.5 s (`StrainActivationCfg` defaults, with `stress_filter_time = 0.1`). The compaction above moves at 0.3 cm/s or less, so sand is allowed to sleep while still compacting. In the press test below, a bed with sleeping on stopped sinking within 2 s (surface at -4.46 cm after 2, 12 and 24 s) while the awake bed went on from -4.62 to -5.10 cm.
- **Lowering the threshold is not the fix.** To let the bed finish first it would have to be below about 0.01 cm/s, and then little would ever sleep. The fix is to remove the compaction (sampling, point a) or to settle for a fixed, longer time with everything awake before sleeping is switched on; T3's own table shows the result stops changing from about 6 s.
- **Press test** (`indent_*.json`, same bed, box 0.2 x 0.2 m pressed 10 cm at 0.1 m/s, one run each):

  | Sleeping | Settled before the press | Surface [cm] | Fz at 2 / 6 / 10 cm [N] | Mean Fz while held [N] |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | off | 2 s | -4.62 | 190 / 425 / 867 | 636 |
  | off | 12 s | -5.01 | 186 / 472 / 925 | 729 |
  | off | 24 s | -5.10 | 189 / 475 / 908 | 729 |
  | on | 2 s | -4.46 | 213 / 526 / 941 | 749 |
  | on | 12 s | -4.46 | 203 / 507 / 918 | 767 |
  | on | 24 s | -4.46 | 214 / 488 / 987 | 764 |

  A sleeping bed is 5 to 18% stiffer under the box than an awake bed, and an awake bed is about 15% stiffer in the hold after 12 s than after 2 s. This is the same direction as T3's finding but far smaller than its factor of 5 in foot sinkage. The box is driven in position; a foot is driven by load and steps repeatedly on sand it has just woken, which I did not test.
- **Why sleeping sand is stiffer (from the code, not tested):** only the patches near a moving collider wake (default margin two voxels, 8 cm), and the layer around them is held as static support. The awake sand under a foot is therefore a small pocket with fixed walls. A wider wake margin (`collider_margin`) should bring it towards the awake result; T3's "all sand near the robot awake" setting is the limit of that.
- **State sand should be in before sleeping:** at its final density. With the present sampling that state is reached only asymptotically, so the practical rule is a fixed awake settling time for every bed and every task, stated with each result.

### c. How the cache's settled template relates

The template is one 0.2 m tile settled for 2.5 s between frictionless walls with 100 iterations and tolerance 1e-6 (`settle_template` in `particle_cache.py`). It uses the same 1.25 particles per edge, so it compacts by the same mechanism, and it has had 2.5 s to do it where a full bed in the T1 runs had 1 s. That is consistent with the earlier measurement that fresh cached sand lies about 1 cm lower and is 43 to 57% stiffer in drag. So the template is not a different material; it is the same sand further along its compaction, and mirrored tile by tile. Not tested: how much of the 43 to 57% is compaction and how much is the mirrored tiling.

### d. What this means for T1's merged results

- **Cost results (terrain size, environment count, sleeping rows): no change.** They are memory and step time, which do not depend on the bed's density. If the tasks moved to 1.6 or 2 particles per edge, every particle count would rise 2.1 or 4.1 times for full and streaming alike; the ratios between modes were not measured at that sampling.
- **Terrain-history heights: conclusion stands, with a caveat.** Heights are taken relative to each mode's surface after a 2 s hover, and an awake bed sinks about another 0.5 to 1 cm over the following laps. That drift is the size of the reported 0.96 cm rms (cache against full) and 0.83 cm (repeated full run), so differences below about 1 cm cannot be attributed to the cache. The 4.97 cm loss without the cache is well above it.
- **Terrain-history forces: the ratios stand, the absolute values are specific to the discretization.** T8 shows the drag on this box runs from -31 to -150 N with voxel size and sampling for the same material preset. The merged statement compares modes at one setting (voxel 4 cm, 1.25 per edge), which is what it supports.
- **"With sleeping on, the cache's force does not match full" and "both streaming modes are softer than full on fresh sand":** both fit this mechanism (sleeping stops compaction at different moments in the two modes; streamed sand is generated later than the full bed and has compacted for less time), but I did not test either link.
- **Suggested caveat for `FINAL.md`** (for the lead to place; I have not edited it): "The simulated bed compacts after it is generated (about 15% in density at the task's 1.25 particles per cell edge) and keeps sinking slowly while awake. Height differences below about 1 cm and force differences between modes with different awake times are within this effect. Absolute forces are specific to the voxel size and particle sampling (T8)."

### e. Why the box force falls as the voxel shrinks

Three contributions, none isolated by a test of mine:

- **The box acts larger on a coarse grid.** The collider enters the solve through grid nodes (`collider_basis = "S2"`, collider volume integrated per node), and particle contacts switch on within 0.25 voxel of the surface (`Config.collider_basis` docstring). T8's margin test supports this: shrinking the box by half a voxel removes 35% of the force at 4 cm and 15% at 2 cm. If the box is effectively half a voxel larger on each face, its buried front grows from 21 x 6.5 cm at a 1 cm voxel to 24 x 8 cm at 4 cm; with resistance rising with width and with depth squared, that alone is a factor of about 1.7 of the measured 2.0.
- **The sheared zone is as thick as the grid allows.** Velocity is trilinear per cell (`velocity_basis = "Q1"`), so the sand dragged along with the box extends about one cell from it, and more sand is moved on a coarse grid.
- **The jitter is fixed at 5 cm,** so it is 1.25 voxels at 4 cm and 5 voxels at 1 cm: more empty cells and more compaction on the fine grid (table in a), which changes the sand as well as the resolution.

**What does not port cleanly.**
1. **`full` is now a different task from the streaming modes.** The old probe built `full` from the patch task with the patch pinned, so all three modes shared one code path. In the unified code `full` is the fixed-bed task, with its own environment class and scene. Consequences that remain after the port: the bed is bounded by a rigid floor and 0.2 m walls instead of a ring of kinematic particles; its sand is settled for 1.0 s and restored from a snapshot on reset; and its force reading adds contact with the rigid floor to the sand force (zero unless the box reaches the floor).
2. **The two tasks' sand settings differ** (voxel, material, depth; see the table above). The probe overrides the fixed bed with the streaming task's sand layer. The fixed-bed physics preset takes its solver voxel size from the streaming scene file while its particles are sampled with the fixed-bed scene's own value, so the probe sets both explicitly.
3. **No fixed-bed preset matches the streaming presets' sleep rule.** The probe adds strain activation inside a region on top of `solver_cfg_all_particle`.
4. **The presets name `pelvis`** for the pool and for the region; the probe replaces both by `box`.
5. **Corridors only work with one environment in the streaming modes.** The environments share one terrain mesh and are placed on a square grid with a single spacing, so with several environments each gets a square of the longer side. Step 2 uses one environment and step 3 uses 8 x 8 m, so neither is affected.
6. **Sparse-grid node capacities are fixed per world** in the task configuration. They were sufficient in the smoke runs; the long `full` corridors may need them raised, by override.

## A. Scope agreed with Junnosuke (not yet recorded in `DECISIONS.md` or `tasks/T1.md`)

**T1 uses a primitive probe only: a box on a PD-driven gantry, with no robot and no policy.** T1 evaluates the terrain representation; robot results belong to later tasks. The probe gives prescribed trajectories, so force and sinkage can be compared across modes without gait divergence. Fx, Fy and Fz come from the coupled solver's per-body force buffer.

**Modes (same task family, material, voxel 0.04 m, sand depth 0.25 m, timestep; only domain management differs):**
- `full`: the patch spans the whole bed with its center pinned, so nothing is recycled. This is the reference.
- `recycle_nocache`: the 1.2 x 1.2 m patch follows the tracked body; terrain that re-enters is reset to its initial state. **Proposed primary method.**
- `recycle_cache`: the same patch plus the particle cache. Optional upgrade; see the cost evidence in section B.

**Results T1 will show**
1. Cost vs traversed distance (peak VRAM, step time mean/p95, physics time per step), all modes.
2. Cost vs number of environments: VRAM per env, time per step per env, largest env count that fits.
3. Fidelity: Fx, Fy, Fz time series and sinkage against `full`, for an indentation and a constant-speed drag.
4. Revisit: drag out and back along the same track; height-map and force error on the return pass for all three modes.
5. Cost of the cache: extra VRAM per env, and the limit on revisitable area (derived from 1 and 2).

Sinkage = depth of the body's lowest point below the undisturbed sand surface at the body position, positive downward. As implemented in the probe, the surface height is the median top-of-particle height in a 0.6 x 0.6 m window under the box after a 2 s pre-settle in the same run. ## E. Caveats
- One trial per configuration and one seed; no confidence intervals yet.
- Section B uses a robot, which T1 will not; treat it as supplementary.
- In `full` mode, particles reaching the 0.2 m boundary ring become kinematic, a small difference from a free bed.

The +9 ms step time and +320 MiB VRAM cannot be attributed to caching alone. The primitive probe removes (1) (one rigid body), but (2) and (3) are part of how the cache is built; they should be reported as separate factors, for example by also running `recycle_nocache` with the graph disabled.
- Probe cost sweeps are one run per point. The G1 env-count sweep (supplementary) is still running on the 5080 and is not on the slides yet. The indentation numbers come from a single RTX 5090 development run of one mode. The out-and-back videos were rendered on the 5090, not the 5080, one run per mode.
- The largest environment counts that fit are specific to this 16 GB card, to D = 4 m, and to this probe (one small rigid body); a robot task has more per-env state, so it would fit fewer.
- The probe's vertical axis is position-controlled, so sinkage follows the commanded depth. A constant-load (force-controlled) press would make sinkage an outcome; it is not built.

## 6. Failed attempts: what was tried and did not work, and why.

Historical failures/limitations below are supported by local notes/results or conversation reports, not rerun now:

- Comparing GUI FPS across the two RTX paths was misleading: main presented DLSS intermediate frames while the experimental window selected the latest image. Earlier headless RTX comparisons also did not request an image each step and were invalid as interactive-rendering benchmarks.
- Enabling captured physics alone did not eliminate cached-patch latency spikes; the early eager/captured p95 values remained roughly 0.67–0.69 s/control step.
- Inactive reserve slots left near the patch center/world Z=0 entered active grid cells even when flagged inactive. The diagnostic attributes expensive FEM integration loops to position-based binning; parking reserve slots outside the active domain reduced spikes. The `cache_feet_only` diagnostic was worse (mean 356.48 ms/control step), so restricting to feet was not sufficient. These contaminated timings are not a controlled benchmark.
- Per-material template generation caused expensive randomized resets. Current main notes describe reusing one initial-material reference template instead; this trades initialization fidelity for eliminating repeated settling.
- Experimental two-second dune settling exposed escaping particles and plastic-volume overflow. Notes say nonfinite snapshots were rejected and one-second preparation passed. Longer relaxation was not demonstrated safe/equilibrated.
- Surface rendering could grow expensive with visited area and had background seams/visibility problems. Subsequent implementations restrict or cache meshes and filter particle display; these are rendering changes, not force-fidelity validation.
- Merely configuring a small compact working set is unsafe when startup/reset wakes the whole resident bed. The documented default retained full evaluation capacity; no proven large VRAM reduction follows from sleeping alone.

## 7. Known bugs and open issues.

- No matched cost/fidelity/cache benchmark satisfying T1 acceptance criteria has been located. Do not present the historical diagnostic tables as satisfying them.
- Task/dependency dirty trees must be preserved before a clean worktree can reproduce this state. No commit was made in this handoff step.
- Some old task imports and documentation paths are stale after the `velocity_mpm/` relocation. Experimental top-level documentation predates later paged-region work; prefer current task source and its README for feature ownership.
- Same checkpoint/seed is not identical robot motion. The eager/captured trajectory mismatch already demonstrates this confound. Matching force/sinkage requires prescribed trajectory replay or another explicitly controlled protocol.
- Cached unloaded material is frozen; it cannot reproduce ongoing remote avalanches, creep or stress redistribution. A settled flat reference template is approximate on slopes and after material randomization.
- Experimental finite-bed body regions do not bound resident particle memory. The generated paged adapter is documented as supporting one environment; multi-object region selection is not proof of multi-environment paged-adapter support.
- Default moving-region filtering retains moving/changing material outside tracked boxes; the support halo can extend beyond them. A hard cutoff changes the physical approximation. Small active particles near distant edges need not mean the bed is a tracking target.
- Grid activity, particle flags, support constraints, and cached material-history restoration must all remain valid across graph replay/reset. Existing tests cover subsets; full current integration correctness is not reverified.
- Main G1 asset collision filtering and Newton contact fixes can alter the reference. Current asset/backend versions must be held fixed in a new three-mode comparison; archived profile numbers use older states.
- Total host memory, GPU cache occupancy, mesh-pool growth, and paging/synchronization tails over long traveled distances remain unquantified here.
- A terrain-relative sinkage definition is still needed: measurement point on the foot, undeformed/reference surface height at that point, contact inclusion rule, and sign convention. Raw ankle height or an isosurface rendering is not automatically physical sinkage.
