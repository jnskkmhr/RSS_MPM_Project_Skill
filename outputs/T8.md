**The common depth rule.** With `M(z)` the mass per area of the particles (MPM) or grains (DEM) whose centre lies above height `z`, inside the strip from 0.2 m behind the start of the drag to 0.2 m beyond its end and 0.3 m to either side of the centre line: take `zA` 5 cm and `zB` 13 cm below the highest-centre surface, the bulk density `rho = (M(zB) - M(zA)) / 8 cm`, and the surface `zA + M(zA) / rho`. This is the height at which a bed of uniform density `rho` would end. It uses only masses and centres, so it does not depend on particle size, unlike the highest particle centre (the probe's rule, used for MPM in the first round) or the highest grain top (used for DEM in the first round). The analysis recomputes it from the saved particles of every run (`analysis/compare.py`, `depth_measures`), independently of the value used in the run:

**Differences that remain between the two runs.**
1. The MPM box is dynamic (PD gantry): during the drag it lags the target by 1.5 cm in x and its underside is 6.13 cm below the surface at 6 cm commanded (DEM 6.04 cm). At the end of the hold after lowering it still presses on the sand with 222 N; the DEM box carries 2 N.
2. The MPM force is one sample per 0.02 s control step; the DEM force is the mean of 10 samples per 0.02 s.
3. The MPM bed is not flat and not at rest before the drag (P2); the DEM bed is flat and at rest.
4. The beds are prepared differently (MPM: generated in place and settled for 2 s; DEM: deposited under gravity and settled for 2 s).
5. The DEM force is still rising through the averaging window, the MPM force is not (P4).
6. The MPM box presses on the sand before the drag and the DEM box does not. At task settings the 222 N at the end of the hold fall to 17 N within 5 cm of drag and the box sits 0.09 cm below its target in the window, worth 2 to 3% of the force (`checks/T8.md`, check 2; not recomputed here). In the MPM runs with 2 to 5 particles per cell edge the box carries 400 to 2088 N at the end of the hold (from the force traces), starts the drag up to 2 cm short of its depth and reaches it after about 0.2 m of drag (check 2); the averaging window starts at 0.5 m, so the reported forces are at depth, but the first 0.2 m of those runs is a different test.
7. The four MPM beds of the main case share one grid alignment. Shifting the bed in z by a fraction of a voxel changed the force by +12 to +19% at 4 cm and -6 to +13% at 2 cm (one run each; not run at 3 cm), so the bed-to-bed spread understates the MPM uncertainty.

Neither sampling is a validated reference: the task-sampling sand creeps from 20° and compacts from 640 to about 700 to 780 kg/m3 while settling, and the densely sampled sand holds slopes of 42.5° and more, above its own friction angle of 36.1°. The dense beds are also lighter above the box because they do not compact, so the comparison is at equal geometric depth, not equal overburden (noted in `checks/T8.md`, check 2). Hypothesis, not a measured result: that the 4 cm task-sampling value lies nearer DEM A than finer voxels do because a coarse voxel raises the force while sparse sampling lowers it; DEM A was also calibrated to the tilt angle of the task-sampling sand, so some closeness is by construction. A change of voxel or particle count changes the terrain a policy sees. These statements are about one 0.2 m box at 6 cm depth and 0.5 m/s; feet, other depths and speeds were not tested.

## Not done / caveats

- Neither simulator is the truth and there is no experiment here. DEM with 9 to 20 mm clumps is itself a model; its force also falls with grain size and has not levelled off.
- The DEM material was calibrated to the MPM sand at task sampling, which is weaker than the preset (slides at 32.5 deg by the 1 cm/s rule, preset 36 deg). Against better-sampled MPM, or with the 1 cm/s rule replaced, another DEM material would be the match (material B: -112 N).
- The MPM force has no converged value in these runs: -31 to -165 N over the 55 MPM runs at one depth (box margin runs left out). Any force ratio quoted is for one voxel size and one particle sampling.
- Not done: a solver convergence record; well-sampled MPM at voxels below 2 cm (beyond 3 particles per edge at 1.5 cm); shifts of the bed in x and y; repeats of the resolution runs; other speeds, box sizes and materials; a pile test; DEM with millimetre grains.
- Every point of the voxel, sampling, one-setting and depth series, the 9, 11 and 20 mm DEM runs, both long-bed runs and every MPM tilt run is a single run at one grid alignment; with z shifts moving the force by up to 19%, differences below about 20% between single MPM runs are not established.
- The second round has not been checked by a second agent. All runs were made in one unattended session on 2026-11-24.
- The MPM box is a 10 kg body on a PD gantry: it sits 0.1 cm lower than commanded and presses on the sand before the drag; the DEM box is kinematic.
- The MPM bed is not flat at its side walls and keeps sinking a little during the run; the DEM bed is flat and at rest.
- The MPM code is the stack archive of 2026-11-22 (IsaacLab a60612c79 with 69 uncommitted files, Newton e681453d), not the code of the T1 run (IsaacLab 65093be1c, Newton 6fee8b0b) and not today's tree. The wall height and spawner details were read from today's tree.
- The MPM runs with jitter 0.5 spacing or no jitter at a 4 cm voxel keep their lattice layers, so the common surface is ill-defined there (measured depth 6.36 cm for jitter 0.5); their +71% and -150 N are not clean comparisons.
- Before the drag the MPM box presses on the sand: 222 N at task settings, 400 to 2088 N in the runs with more particles per cell, where the box starts the drag up to 2 cm short of its depth and reaches it after about 0.2 m (checks/T8.md, check 2). The averaging window starts at 0.5 m.

Failures of the second round: none of the 97 tasks failed. The solver residual printout produced no output. First-round failures are listed in `results/round1/T8_round1.md`.
