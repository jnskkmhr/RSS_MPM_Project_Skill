## Issues
Each with what is wrong and what is needed to fix it.

1. **The headline sweep and its explanation are on different beds, and the explanation does not hold on the bed of the headline.** Claim A is on the 0.35 m bed; claims E and F (sand motion, sole angle, failure types) are on the 0.25 m bed, where by the write-up's own rule the cells that matter are invalid: at +30° 15, 19 and 20 of 20 trials stand on the base (MJWarp, RFT, MPM), at +20° 17 of 20 for MJWarp. On the 0.35 m bed (`geo35_1/slope/`, stance records exist for MJWarp and RFT only):
   - Failure types change. MJWarp's 35 failed uphill trials are 18 falls forward, 13 collapses, 3 lane exits, 1 fall sideways. "11 left the lane sideways, all MJWarp" and "its yaw error matches leaving the lane" describe the shallow bed only.
   - Sand under MJWarp's sole is not the same as under RFT's: 5.4 against 3.3 cm at +20°, 8.2 against 4.7 at +25°, 8.9 against 6.7 at +30° (MJWarp is falling in these cells). RFT's values are unchanged by the bed (3.3 against 3.4, 6.7 against 6.9 cm).
   - MJWarp's sole angle at +20° is 60° toes-down instead of 48°.
   - The MPM run on the 0.35 m bed was made without `--foot_sand`, so there is no stance record, sole angle or sand motion for MPM on that bed.
   Needed: rerun MPM on the 0.35 m bed with `--foot_sand` in the same workflow as the baselines, and rebuild the explanation block and its slides (sand motion, sole angle, failure counts, close-up) from that bed. Until then, label E and F as 0.25 m results in which the feet reached the base at +30° for every policy and from +20° for MJWarp.
2. **MPM against RFT rests on one cell, and that cell is invalid by the write-up's rule.** On the 0.35 m bed MPM and RFT differ only at +30° (10 against 4 of 10; 2 of 10 in the other baseline run). There 4 of 10 MPM trials and 6 of 10 RFT trials come within 2 cm of the base. At +25°, the steepest cell valid for both, it is 10 against 9, inside the scatter of claim B. The first slide bullet ("only MPM reaches every goal up to 30°") includes the invalid cell. MPM against MJWarp is solid: 10 against 0 of 10 at +20° and +25° in two runs (MJWarp's own cells there are marked invalid for 1 and 3 trials, MPM's are valid), 10 against 6 and 3 at +15°. Needed: a bed deep enough that +30° is valid (the 0.35 m clearance at +25° is already down to 4.5 to 5.5 cm), or state the MPM-against-RFT result as "not separated up to +25°".
3. **Provenance is missing from the P1 blocks, and the compared runs are not one run.** None of the current P1 blocks states commit, dirty tree, command or seed; GPU and task are only in the superseded block. In the result files the `code` field is empty and the seed is not stored (it is in the logs: 41). The facts exist in `PROVENANCE_*.txt`: IsaacLab `a60612c79`, 73 dirty entries; Newton `e681453d`, 1 dirty entry. Because the tree is dirty and `scripts/benchmarks/t3/` is untracked, the same commit does not mean the same code: the MPM run of claim A is from 11-23 18:05, the baseline run from 11-24 00:49, and `eval_walk.py` was last changed 11-24 00:03. The write-up does say the runs are from two days. The earlier baseline run (11-23 18:38) gives the same picture, which limits the damage. Needed: one workflow with all three policies per result, the bundle (or a patch of the dirty tree) archived per workflow, and commit, dirty state, command, seed and GPU written in each block.
4. **The 2 cm validity rule does not separate results that the base affects.** On the three soft sands of the material axis MJWarp's velocity error on level ground is 0.10 to 0.12 m/s on the 0.25 m bed (closest approach 2.2 to 2.8 cm, two of the three cells counted valid) and 0.29 to 0.35 m/s on the 0.35 m bed (`flow_1/material_axis/`, `flow35_1/material_axis/`, sands 2000 kg/m³ with friction 0.50, 1000 with 0.70, 500 with 0.90). A cell that passes the rule changed threefold when the bed got deeper. On the 0.35 m grid the three sands where MJWarp's error is high (0.21, 0.33, 0.33 m/s) are the valid sands with the smallest clearance (4.6, 3.5, 3.3 cm). Other depth effects in cells the rule passes or nearly passes: MPM at −30° 0.69 against 0.35 m/s; MJWarp at +15° 8 against 6 and 3 of 10. The rule was also set after the results were seen (11-24 00:30; the MPM run and the grid are from 11-23), which measurement rule 2 asks to avoid. ## Method

**M1. The flow in litres is a mass measure, and overstates the volume that left (a).** Flow is the particle count times `particle_volume_m3`, which is the median particle mass divided by the material density (`eval_walk.py:557`), so it is mass-weighted and the sparse sampling does not under-count it. But the settled bed is denser than that: the bed base is at −0.50 m and the settled surface at −0.08 to −0.15 m, and from counts and heights at touchdown each particle occupies 0.67–0.82 of its nominal volume in W (0.70–0.80 in T). The identity is rut = (particles lost × bulk volume per particle) + compaction. Using the nominal volume overstates the first term by 22–50 % and understates compaction by the same litres. With the bulk volume:

| W | Column: flow as reported → by bulk volume [L] | Column: compaction share of the rut, reported → corrected |
|---|---|---|
| Dense sand | 0.16–0.18 → 0.12–0.13 | 49–50 % → 62–63 % |
| Beach sand | 0.33–0.39 → 0.24–0.28 | 16–36 % → 39–54 % |
| Packed poppy seed | 0.49–0.83 → 0.38–0.66 | 20–44 % → 37–57 % |
| Light sand | 0.79–1.08 → 0.53–0.84 | −5–22 % → 26–48 % |
| Loose poppy seed | 1.33–1.53 → 0.94–1.03 | 1–10 % → 33–39 % |

The column values "0.00 ± 0.09" and "0.01 ± 0.04 L" of compaction on light sand and loose poppy seed, and the negative ones in T, are artefacts of this mismatch, not a sign that the rut there is pure flow. Comparisons between policies on one ground and all rank orders are not affected (one factor per ground). The table should either say "litres at the material's nominal density" or use the bulk volume.

**M2. The compaction is a real rise in particles per volume (a).** Checked directly from counts and sand-top heights, independent of the litre conversion: per footprint the column gets 4–5 % denser on dense sand, 10–14 % on packed poppy seed and light sand and 17–20 % on loose poppy seed in W; the 39 by 27 cm region 3 % to 15 %. The column and the region run to the bed base, so sand that moved down and outward at depth is counted as flow, not as compaction. The sand top is a 97th percentile, taken the same way before and after, with airborne sand excluded; on a strongly pitched rut (MJWarp, −24° to −48°) it reads the high end of the rut and understates the drop. A 10–20 % density rise through a 36 cm column of rigid grains in one step is not physical; it is the sampling compaction T1 describes, on a bed that is already 1.2–1.5 times its nominal density when the foot arrives.

**M3. The budget closes by construction; the control region is not untouched (a).** Compaction is defined as sank − rose − left, so there is no residual to report and no independent closure test in the saved arrays. Two points on the control region 0.35 m to the side:
- In W it sinks 0.44–0.85 L between touchdown and the next landing of the same foot (4–8 mm) and loses almost no particles. The undisturbed surface 0.6 m ahead of the pelvis does not drift over a trial (dense sand: −9.7 to −10.2 cm throughout), so this is not the bed settling by itself with time; it happens as the robot passes. "The bed also compacts by itself" is not shown. The step's compaction lies between the reported value (control taken off: 0.45–3.4 L) and the raw one (1.0–4.1 L).
- In T the control region **gains** 0.2–0.6 L of sand by count on every sand except loose poppy seed: sand pushed out of the footprint region arrives there. Taking the control off therefore adds this gain to the region's flow (raw 0.67–2.32 L leave the region; reported 0.91–2.79 L) and removes it from compaction. The T budget values are biased towards flow by 0.2–0.6 L.
The region also contains the far-field of the other foot, which lands 3 cm outside it during the interval.

## Judgement of the interpretation

**J1. The three terms sum by construction; the two quoted ones do not sum to the quoted total.** Whole-stance foot motion = motion during the first 0.06 s + sand motion + slide, and the slide is the remainder, not a measurement of its own. At +20°: RFT 0.7 + 3.6 + 0.3 = 4.5; MPM −1.5 + 2.2 + 3.4 = 4.2. "Of that [4.5 and 4.2 cm], the sand carries 3.6 against 2.2 cm" is wrong for MPM: sand plus slide is 5.7 cm, more than the 4.2 cm, because MPM's foot is still moving 1.5 cm uphill while it digs in. After dig-in MPM's bearing point moves 5.7 cm downhill against RFT's 3.8 cm. The equal whole-stance values (4.5 and 4.2) are a cancellation, and in W they are not equal (3.9 against 1.3 cm).

**J2. Same sand patch: yes.** The slide is taken against the particles marked 0.06 s after touchdown and followed by index to the last loaded sample. The layer 3–8 cm under the sole moves 0.5 cm less than the marked layer for both policies (3.1 against 3.6, 1.7 against 2.2 cm at +20°), so shear inside the marked layer is small against the slide.

**J3. The slide is the toe's path, and it happens while the foot is nearly unloaded.**
- The bearing point is the toe in every uphill stance (lowest of heel, middle, toe: toe in 100 % of intervals from +5°). Between dig-in and the last loaded sample the sole pitches further toes-down by 15° under RFT (28° to 43° at +20°) and by 30° under MPM (29° to 58°). ## (a) Is the cap applied as claimed, and only to the knees

**Yes.** `eval_walk.py` replaces the `.*_knee_joint` entry of `joint_effort_limit` of the `legs` actuator group in the robot configuration before the environment is built, and nothing else. Newton's MuJoCo solver writes the effort limit as the joint's `actfrcrange`, a symmetric clamp on the summed actuator force (P and D terms). Read back in every run: the ten other leg joints hold 88 / 139 / 88 / 50 / 50 N m in all 50 workflows (one distinct set). Gains: the gain-scaling event is in the list of disabled events of every run and the configuration is not touched. The action is a joint position target with the fixed scale 0.25 and the default offset; no observation, action or termination term of the task reads the effort limit, and the policy does not observe torque. Observation and action scaling are not affected.

**No artefact on level ground.** Every trial starts with 1.87 m of level sand before the slope. All 290 capped trials cross it and get at least 1.15 m past the start of the slope (70 N m; 2.12 m at 89 N m). On the level part the knee torque is the same with and without the cap (95th percentile 30 to 32 N m) and a knee is at the cap in 0.1 to 2.2 % of the samples. Mild slope with the cap: +20° at 89 N m, 40 of 40, velocity error 0.085 against 0.070 m/s. At 70 N m: level part walked in all 80 trials, +25° 24 of 40. There is no 70 N m cell at +20° or on level ground over a full trial.

**What the cap does on the slope.** The failures are not a knee that buckles straight down: none ends by the height limit, all by the forward tilt. The posture does drift toward a crouch as the cap goes down (mean knee angle on the slope 70, 73, 75, 78, 90° at 139, 118, 104, 89, 70 N m; RFT 78°), the pelvis is low before the fall as in RFT's falls (0.26 to 0.28 m above the surface 1.0 to 0.3 s before the end; RFT's falls 0.29 m; trials that reach the goal 0.37 to 0.39 m), and the falling trials run ahead of the command (1.06 to 1.08 m/s; RFT's 1.08). ## D. Judgement

1. **"The slope result holds on a planar slope" follows by the committed rule.** The three success verdicts are the tile's (not separated at +20° and +25°, MPM better at +30°). The rule is fair for that statement and weak for the second one. "No change shown" per cell is a test that, with 40 trials and p below 0.01, would have reported a change for RFT at +30° only at 2 or fewer or 26 or more goals against the tile's 13. The planar minus valley difference in RFT's success at +30° is +12.5 points, 95 % interval -9 to +32 points (Newcombe; Wald -9 to +34). So the data do not show that the valley has no effect on RFT; they show that an effect larger than about 30 points is unlikely and that it cannot remove the gap to MPM (40 of 40). Write "no terrain effect shown; RFT 18 against 13 of 40, difference +12 points, 95 % interval -9 to +32", not "the valley does not matter". The velocity-error verdict at +30° does differ between the terrains; the rule did not cover it, the table shows it.
2. **MJWarp: simulation against hardware.** On a planar slope, which is what the hardware slope is, the MJWarp policy reaches 7 of 40 goals at +10°, 0 of 40 at +15° and 0 of 40 at +20° in this simulation. On hardware every policy, the rigid-trained one included, succeeded on a 10° slope of real sand. The planar runs remove the valley as an excuse: the disagreement is between this MPM evaluation and the real robot. Either the simulated beach sand on a 0.50 m bed is harder to climb than the real sand, or the trial differs (1.0 m/s command, 5 m of slope in 13 s, start with a 10 cm drop, feet-only sand contact), or the hardware policy is not this checkpoint; none of these was checked here. Until it is resolved, the simulated MJWarp slope numbers cannot be presented as a prediction of what a rigid-trained policy does on a real slope, and the paper has to state the disagreement.
3. **Standing start on a planar 30° ramp: recorded as local trials.** Verified in `logs/t3/final_1/long_check/ramp30_n3_rft_deep` (falls at 1.48, 1.80, 1.50 s) and `ramp30_n3_mpm_retrain` (three time-outs at 23 s), one seed, three robots. `outputs/T3.md` calls it "tried locally" and uses it only to justify the level approach; the neighbouring 56 m note says "These local trials are not results". The sentence on the standing start itself should carry the same words; it must not reach the slides or `FINAL.md` as a finding.
4. **`FINAL.md`**: text below.
5. **Slide.** The second line "Same result on a planar slope: at +30° MPM climbs, RFT often falls" is supported (40 against 18 of 40) and tagged unchecked; the collapsed table matches the data cell by cell. Corrections: (a) the conditions line is repeated on the sand-flow, sole and whole-body slides with "The same runs on a planar slope give the same result"; on the planar ramp only goals, failure kind, velocity error and sand under the sole were compared, none of those slides' analyses was repeated, so there it should read "On a planar slope the three policies' success is the same (slope sweep slide); the analyses on this slide were not repeated there"; (b) "the lane is 3 m wide" is the side limit of 1.5 m; (c) formula angles as in A; (d) give the interval of item 1 beside "no change shown" for RFT at +30°.
