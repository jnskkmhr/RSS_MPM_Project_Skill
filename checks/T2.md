## Issues
Each with what is wrong and what is needed to fix it.

1. **The MPM-sand towing rows are on a bed where the robot's feet reach the rigid base.** On the 0.25 m bed the lowest sole corner comes within 2 cm of the base in 16 of 16 towing trials (1.0 to 1.1 cm at the lowest; 3% of samples at 0.5 m/s, 15% at 1.0 m/s). This is the criterion by which T3 withdrew loose poppy seed on the 0.25 m bed for walking. The pelvis rides 0.51 m above the origin on MPM sand against 0.69 m on rigid ground. On the 0.35 m bed (final checkpoint) the same test gives 119.5 N and 130.9 N instead of 104.6 N and 116.2 N, a cube speed of 0.71 instead of 0.90 m/s, 13.6 s to the goal instead of 11.2 s, and no start-up peak (ratio 1.02 against 1.11); 3 of 16 trials are still within 2 cm and all 16 within 3 cm. So "105 N on MPM sand" and "about twice the pull of rigid ground" are properties of the 0.25 m bed, not of the sand. The MPM tow policy was also trained on the 0.25 m bed. The logs behind the reported tables hold no foot positions, so the reported runs themselves cannot be checked. *Needed:* report towing on a bed where no trial comes within 2 cm (a 0.45 m run had started: `t2/tow_d45/raw`, one file); log foot positions in every tow run; state the bed depth in every table and caption; say in the write-up that the policy was trained on the shallow bed, or retrain.

2. **Mixed checkpoints, and the final checkpoint changes cells.** Every reported table pairs MPM checkpoint 30,500 (10,501 tow iterations) with baselines at 39,998 (19,999). `T2-TOW-DIAG` uses yet other policies (`g1-tow-rigid-2`, `g1-tow-soft-contact-1`, MPM 21,500), and `outputs/T2.md` uses 21,500 and 22,500. With MPM 39,998: on matched RFT sand at 0.5 m/s 16 / 16 instead of 8 / 16 (so the slide's "the MPM-trained policy is not best everywhere ... 8 of 16" no longer holds); with RFT surface friction 0.73, 14 / 16 at 1.0 m/s; with the tether cut at 1.0 m/s it stays up in 15 / 16 on the 0.25 m bed and 9 / 16 on the 0.35 m bed and on RFT sand, so "stays up in 32 of 32" holds for 30,500 on the shallow bed only. The newer runs are 2 x 8 trials with seeds 42 and 43, not the 16 trials of seed 42, so they are not the same start states. *Needed:* one set of tables on the three final checkpoints, one bed depth, one RFT material, the same trial layout in every cell, and the write-up naming the folders; drop or relabel the 30,500 tables.

3. **What the logs do and do not support about the baselines.** Supported: their 0 of 16 on MPM sand is not a towing-force result. They end at the 45° tilt limit at 0.9 to 1.1 s with the tether cut and at 1.1 to 1.45 s with it, in 16 of 16 trials, on both beds; the tether goes taut at about 0.55 s and delays the end by 0.2 to 0.35 s. Not supported: "cannot walk on the sand". No baseline trial reaches steady walking. Each trial starts with a drop: the sand top is 5.35 cm under the level the robot is placed on and gives way, the pelvis is 14 cm lower after 0.2 s and, for the baselines, 34 to 40 cm lower after 1 s, from a start with up to 0.5 m/s of velocity and joint angles scaled 0.5 to 1.5. The logs cannot separate "cannot walk on this sand" from "does not survive this reset on this sand". The stated cause (tow training on flat rigid ground removed the ability to walk on sand) rests on `T2-TOW-DIAG`, which used other checkpoints and a 0.25 m bed without foot positions, where T3 found walking feet on the base. The fixed-start control was not run for `g1-tow-rigid-3` and `g1-tow-soft-contact-2`. **(b) Which reading? (ii): the policy is close to its tilt limit on MPM sand and the tether load uses up the margin, the more the heavier the cube. (i) and (iii) are contradicted.**

- Against (i), force beyond what the policy can produce: it produces more elsewhere (71 to 86 N of horizontal pull on rigid ground and RFT sand against 44 to 51 N in the failing 10 kg cells), and on MPM sand it is not stopped: it tows faster than commanded up to the fall. Where the force is really too much for it (rigid ground, 24 kg) it slows to 0.33 m/s and runs out of time; it does not fall.
- Against (iii), tether dynamics on sand: the tension within a trial is no more variable on MPM sand than elsewhere for this policy (standard deviation within a trial from 5 s: 8 to 12 N with 8 and 10 kg on MPM sand, 8 to 15 N on rigid ground, 9 to 15 N on RFT sand; 99th percentile 61 to 88 N against 103 to 130 N in the heavy-cube cells). The tether is never slack. The MPM-trained policy has the larger variation on the same sands (13 to 17 N; 24 to 27 N with 15 kg) and never falls. No sticking and releasing of the cube shows in the MPM-sand cells. (It does show for the MPM-trained policy on RFT sand, see (f).)
- For (ii): largest pelvis pitch in the first 5 s among the trials that stay up, mean per cell (largest single trial), RFT-trained policy. Rigid ground 7 to 12° (18°), RFT sand 9 to 17°. MPM sands: tether cut 22 to 29° (30 to 37°); 8 kg 28 to 35° (34 to 44°); 10 kg 31 to 38° (35 to 44°); 15 kg 41 to 44°. The termination limit is 45° of pelvis tilt, and every failed trial ends at 43 to 45°. So the margin left in the start-up is 16 to 23° without the cube, 10 to 17° with 8 kg, 7 to 14° with 10 kg and 1 to 4° with 15 kg. The MPM-trained policy peaks at 22° without the cube and 25 to 29° with any of the three cubes (largest 32°): its peak does not grow with the mass.
- The falls also go with the start draw, not only the cell: 7 of the 21 falls with 8 and 10 kg are trial 0 of the merged file (it falls in 7 of the 14 cells), 3 are trial 6. The trials of different cells share their starts and are not independent.
- Note on what "fall" is: a trial ends when the pelvis tilt passes 45°. Survivors with 8 kg already reach 43 to 44° in single trials. The counts are counts of crossing that limit in a forward lurch (check 3: base speed rising to 0.8 to 1.1 m/s in the last 0.5 s); whether each would have ended on the ground is not in the data. The limit is the task's own.

**(c) Dose-response within the ground: yes on every sand, strong from 10 to 15 kg, weak per sand from 8 to 10 kg.**

Falls of 16 with 8, 10, 15 kg: dense sand 0, 1, 8; 3000/0.7 0, 0, 15; 2000/0.9 0, 1, 14; 2000/0.7 1, 5, 16; beach sand 0, 3, 15; 1000/0.9 0, 5, 16; packed poppy seed 0, 5, 16. From 8 to 10 kg it is not distinguishable on the three stiffest sands (0 or 1 fall), and on the other four it is 3 to 5 falls against 0 or 1 (p = 0.17, 0.23, 0.04, 0.04); pooled p is about 1e-5, overstated because the cells share their starts. At the same 56 to 60 N the count also varies with the sand (0 and 1 falls on 3000/0.7 and 2000/0.9; 3 to 5 on the four softer or lower-friction ones), so within MPM sand both the load and the sand matter. The steady torso pitch rises with the mass on every sand as well (13 to 15° cut, 21 to 25° with 8 kg, 24 to 28° with 10 kg, 31 to 34° with 15 kg).

**(d) Why is the RFT-trained policy marginal on MPM sand without load? The data points to how it loads the foot, not to deeper sinkage or backward slip.** Checker's own foot measures: sole centre and corners from `feet_pos`, `feet_quat` and the repo's sole offset; "loaded" is a foot force above 150 N; depth against the settled surface read at the start; from 5 s, trials that stay up.

- Net speed without the cube 0.63 to 0.70 m/s on the 7 sands against 0.51 m/s on rigid ground (confirmed, from net displacement).
- No backward slip: the loaded sole moves +0.01 to +0.03 m/s along the heading without the cube and +0.03 to +0.10 m/s with 8 and 10 kg, that is forward, not backward. The MPM-trained policy: -0.01 to +0.01 m/s.
- No deeper sinkage: lowest loaded sole corner 2.0 to 3.4 cm below the surface without the cube, against 2.2 to 4.6 cm for the MPM-trained policy on the same sands.
- What differs is the foot attitude. The RFT-trained policy stands toe-down while loaded: 3 to 5° without the cube, 7 to 11° with 8 and 10 kg, 16 to 19° in the 15 kg survivors; the toe corners are 1.9 to 3.2 cm deep without the cube and 2.7 to 5.4 cm with 8 and 10 kg, the heel corners 0.3 to 2.2 cm and not deeper with the cube (at or above the surface on the three stiffest sands). The MPM-trained policy stands flat to heel-down (-2 to -3° without the cube, +1 to +3° with 8 and 10 kg; heel 2.1 to 4.4 cm, toe 1.3 to 3.4 cm). On rigid ground both policies stand flat (0°).
- One further observation, not a cause: on RFT sand, in the two cells with foot data (18 and 20 kg), the RFT-trained policy's loaded sole moves backward at 0.23 to 0.26 m/s. On MPM sand it does not. A gait learned on a ground where the stance foot slides back would overshoot the speed on a ground where it holds; there is no foot log of this policy walking on RFT sand without the cube, so this is not tested.
- Not in the data: why the toe-down stance arises, and joint torques or actions. The statement that can be made is: on MPM sand the RFT-trained policy walks 26 to 40% faster than commanded, pitched forward 13 to 15°, on its toes, with the toes 2 to 3 cm into the sand and the heels near the surface.

**(e) The paper claim "MPM enables object transport policies on granular terrain by capturing the larger resistive force of sand".**

Supported: (1) MPM sand resists the cube more than rigid ground or RFT sand (15 kg: 83 to 94 N of tension on the 7 sands against 60 and 73 N); (2) only the MPM-trained policy tows on MPM sand (16 of 16 on every sand and with every cube mass); (3) the RFT-trained policy's failure on MPM sand grows with the load (111, 92, 12 of 112 goals with 8, 10, 15 kg). Not supported: that the larger resistive force is why the RFT-trained policy fails. It stays up at 83 to 98 N on rigid ground and RFT sand and falls on MPM sand from 56 N; the cause is how it walks on MPM sand (too fast, pitched forward, on its toes), with the tether load using up a margin that is already small. So "by capturing the larger resistive force" should not stand as the mechanism; "by capturing how sand responds under the feet as well as on the object" is what the data carries. Limits that stay: evaluated in MPM only, one training seed per policy, one cube shape, 0.5 m/s, tilt limit 45°.

## Not verified

## Verdict: pass with caveats

All reported numbers reproduce. The reading "mixed" by the fixed rule is right, and the plain-words reading (not the tension alone, not the ground alone) is right. Caveats: (1) the paper's "because sand resists more" is contradicted as the mechanism, not merely unproven; (2) the comparison is at equal tension, not equal speed, and that must be said; (3) the MPM-trained policy's goals on RFT sand count path, not distance, in this control and in the merged 15 kg cell.
