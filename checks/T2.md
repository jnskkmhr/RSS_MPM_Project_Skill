## Issues
Each with what is wrong and what is needed to fix it.

1. **The MPM-sand towing rows are on a bed where the robot's feet reach the rigid base.** On the 0.25 m bed the lowest sole corner comes within 2 cm of the base in 16 of 16 towing trials (1.0 to 1.1 cm at the lowest; 3% of samples at 0.5 m/s, 15% at 1.0 m/s). This is the criterion by which T3 withdrew loose poppy seed on the 0.25 m bed for walking. The pelvis rides 0.51 m above the origin on MPM sand against 0.69 m on rigid ground. On the 0.35 m bed (final checkpoint) the same test gives 119.5 N and 130.9 N instead of 104.6 N and 116.2 N, a cube speed of 0.71 instead of 0.90 m/s, 13.6 s to the goal instead of 11.2 s, and no start-up peak (ratio 1.02 against 1.11); 3 of 16 trials are still within 2 cm and all 16 within 3 cm. So "105 N on MPM sand" and "about twice the pull of rigid ground" are properties of the 0.25 m bed, not of the sand. The MPM tow policy was also trained on the 0.25 m bed. The logs behind the reported tables hold no foot positions, so the reported runs themselves cannot be checked. *Needed:* report towing on a bed where no trial comes within 2 cm (a 0.45 m run had started: `t2/tow_d45/raw`, one file); log foot positions in every tow run; state the bed depth in every table and caption; say in the write-up that the policy was trained on the shallow bed, or retrain.

2. **Mixed checkpoints, and the final checkpoint changes cells.** Every reported table pairs MPM checkpoint 30,500 (10,501 tow iterations) with baselines at 39,998 (19,999). `T2-TOW-DIAG` uses yet other policies (`g1-tow-rigid-2`, `g1-tow-soft-contact-1`, MPM 21,500), and `outputs/T2.md` uses 21,500 and 22,500. With MPM 39,998: on matched RFT sand at 0.5 m/s 16 / 16 instead of 8 / 16 (so the slide's "the MPM-trained policy is not best everywhere ... 8 of 16" no longer holds); with RFT surface friction 0.73, 14 / 16 at 1.0 m/s; with the tether cut at 1.0 m/s it stays up in 15 / 16 on the 0.25 m bed and 9 / 16 on the 0.35 m bed and on RFT sand, so "stays up in 32 of 32" holds for 30,500 on the shallow bed only. The newer runs are 2 x 8 trials with seeds 42 and 43, not the 16 trials of seed 42, so they are not the same start states. *Needed:* one set of tables on the three final checkpoints, one bed depth, one RFT material, the same trial layout in every cell, and the write-up naming the folders; drop or relabel the 30,500 tables.

3. **What the logs do and do not support about the baselines.** Supported: their 0 of 16 on MPM sand is not a towing-force result. They end at the 45° tilt limit at 0.9 to 1.1 s with the tether cut and at 1.1 to 1.45 s with it, in 16 of 16 trials, on both beds; the tether goes taut at about 0.55 s and delays the end by 0.2 to 0.35 s. Not supported: "cannot walk on the sand". No baseline trial reaches steady walking. Each trial starts with a drop: the sand top is 5.35 cm under the level the robot is placed on and gives way, the pelvis is 14 cm lower after 0.2 s and, for the baselines, 34 to 40 cm lower after 1 s, from a start with up to 0.5 m/s of velocity and joint angles scaled 0.5 to 1.5. The logs cannot separate "cannot walk on this sand" from "does not survive this reset on this sand". The stated cause (tow training on flat rigid ground removed the ability to walk on sand) rests on `T2-TOW-DIAG`, which used other checkpoints and a 0.25 m bed without foot positions, where T3 found walking feet on the base. The fixed-start control was not run for `g1-tow-rigid-3` and `g1-tow-soft-contact-2`. ## Judgement

**(a) Tension definition. The reported number is the magnitude of the rope force, not its horizontal part; the rigid numbers are Coulomb friction, about 10% low.**

- `tension` equals the norm of `rope_force_on_sled` in every cell. The rope rises from the cube to the pelvis at 31 to 34° on rigid ground, RFT sand and the stiffer MPM sands, and at 23 to 28° on the three softest MPM sands (the pelvis is lower). The horizontal part, which is what the ground resists, is 82 to 92% of the tension: rigid 33 to 80 N; RFT while towing 62 to 64 N; MPM 69 to 94 N at equal speed, 114 and 129 N on the two softest.
- Check on rigid ground, friction × (weight − vertical rope pull) against the measured horizontal pull: friction 0.3: 0.3 × (147.2 − 22.9) = 37.3 N against 33.4 N; 0.5: 57.2 against 50.1 N; 1.0: 99.1 against 80.2 N. As tension at the measured rope angle, μ m g / (cos θ + μ sin θ): 44.4, 66.3, 107.3 N against 40.5, 59.8, 93.5 N measured (91, 90, 87%). The measured ratio of horizontal pull to load is 0.27, 0.36, 0.44, 0.52, 0.60, 0.67, 0.74, 0.81 for friction 0.3 to 1.0, that is 90% of the set coefficient at 0.3 falling to 81% at 1.0.
- So yes: the rigid band is Coulomb friction of a 15 kg cube, lightened by the rope's upward pull (23 to 48 N of the 147 N weight). The cube slides the whole time (never below 0.18 m/s). The shortfall of 10 to 19% against the set coefficient was not explained; the cube's height peaks 0.2 to 1.2 cm above rest (rocking on the pull), and the solver's friction model was not examined.
- Because the rope lifts the cube, a 93 N tension at friction 1.0 is 80 N of ground resistance on a cube pressing with 99 N. The same reading applies to every solver: tension overstates the ground's horizontal force by 9 to 21%.

**(b) RFT: the model does act on the cube, but for a sliding cube it reduces to one fixed drag ratio that none of the varied parameters changes. The figure's RFT band is not "the force RFT provides".**

- What the code does. In the RFT tow task the cube is a second intruder in the analytical sand (`sled_physics_callback`, a 6-face box with 5 × 5 points per face, same material arrays as the feet). The rigid plane is 0.5 m below the sand surface (`SUPPORT_DEPTH`) and is never reached: the cube's centre sits 0.5 to 19 mm below its resting height on the surface. The cube's rigid friction (0.5) is therefore irrelevant. The hypothesis "cube on a rigid plane with a fixed friction coefficient" is wrong as a description of the code.
- What comes out is nevertheless a fixed coefficient. The RFT force on each surface element is (scale ξ) × (depth) × (a direction function), with ξ = density × g × (894 μ³ − 386 μ² + 89 μ). The cube sinks until the vertical force carries its weight, so a stiffer sand only means a shallower cube: sinkage 19, 10, 9, 8, 6, 5, 2, 2, 1.4, 1.4, 1.3, 0.8, 0.5 mm as ξ rises 28-fold. The ratio of horizontal to vertical force is set by the direction function alone. Measured while the cube slides faster than 0.3 m/s: 0.56 to 0.60 on all 13 sands with the RFT-trained policy and 0.59 to 0.61 with the MPM-trained one, for density 250 to 3,000 kg/m³, internal friction 0.7 to 1.07 and surface friction 0.7 to 1.07. The surface friction enters only as a cap on the tangential force (`_friction_cone_check`), and a cap of 0.7 or more is above 0.57 and never binds. (Inference from the code, not run: a surface friction below about 0.57 would lower the pull; the task's own default is 0.3.)
- Hence the constant 72 to 73 N: 0.57 × (147 − 37 N of rope lift) = 62 to 64 N horizontal, at a rope angle of 29 to 31°. RFT gives this one value for a 15 kg cube whatever density and friction are set. As a terrain model for the towed object it is a rigid ground with friction 0.57 (rigid ground at 0.6 gives 67.9 N, at 0.7 75.2 N).
- What the figure's RFT band (57 to 75 N, MPM-trained policy) measures instead: how much of the time the cube stands still. The cube is below 0.05 m/s for 21 to 49% of the time on twelve sands; the drag ratio is 0.59 to 0.61 while it slides and 0.28 to 0.40 while it rests, and the mean tension is the time average. The spread across the 13 points is spread in the policy's stop-and-go, not in the sand's resistance. The one point at 75 N (250/0.7) is the only one where the cube is towed, and there 9 of 16 trials fall.
- Why the RFT-trained policy stalls on the denser sands: not because the cube resists more (the sliding ratio is 0.57 to 0.60 on the stalled sands as well). The cube speed follows the RFT stiffness ξ/g in order: 45k, 90k, 105k, 133k: 0.45 to 0.46 m/s; 180k: 0.39; 210k: 0.31; 360k and above: 0.12, 0.10, 0.09, 0.08, 0.08, 0.06, 0.07 m/s. The policy was trained on RFT default material (3,000 kg/m³, internal and surface friction 0.3), ξ/g = 48k; it tows up to about 4 times that stiffness and stalls from about 7 times. On the stalled sands the loaded foot barely penetrates (ankle link at +1.7 to +3.0 cm, so the sole, 3.5 cm below the link in the task's collider, is 0.5 to 1.9 cm into the sand, against 2.7 to 6.8 cm where it tows) and slides backward at 0.29 to 0.41 m/s, against 0.08 to 0.21 m/s where it tows; the robot treads on the spot at 0.11 to 0.18 m/s, upright (torso 3 to 4°). An RFT foot gives a horizontal force only while it moves through the sand, so traction is paid for with slip; why the slip grows with stiffness for this gait was not worked out. The stall is a property of this policy outside the material it was trained on, not a force limit of RFT.

**(c) "MPM sand gives the highest tension": true against the task's own settings, not true against rigid ground as swept.**

- Drag ratio (horizontal pull over cube load): rigid ground 0.27 to 0.81 over the friction sweep, 0.44 at the task's 0.5; RFT 0.57; MPM 0.68 to 0.97 on the ten sands at equal speed, 1.2 and 1.4 on the two softest.

Reading:

- The stalled robot keeps stepping at its usual rate but with a stride of 2 to 6 cm instead of 22 cm, upright, 12 to 14° inside the free lean band. It marks time. The stance foot does creep back 1 to 2.6 cm per step, but this creep is 2 to 5 cm/s at every force ratio from 0 to 0.15, where the feet spend 78 to 93% of loaded stance on beach sand and 63 to 69% on loose poppy seed; it does not grow with the horizontal force until the ratio exceeds 0.2 (then 3 to 16 cm/s; 1 to 6% of loaded stance on beach sand, 14 to 20% on loose poppy seed). So the feet are not sliding because the sand gives way under the push; most of the time the baselines are not pushing.
- Treading on one spot digs the feet in: with the cube the baselines' soles stand 1 to 7 cm deeper than without it, and deeper than `her588wk`'s on the same sand (15 kg: 1.9 to 12.0 cm against 1.6 to 8.9 cm). `her588wk`'s depth hardly changes with the cube. Deeper feet are at least in part a consequence of not advancing onto fresh sand; whether they also hinder the next stride cannot be told from these logs.
- The sand's traction is not exhausted. On the same sand, under the same vertical load, `her588wk`'s feet carry a ratio of 0.2 to 0.5 for 58% of loaded stance at 15 kg (beach sand) while moving 1.5 cm/s or less, and deliver 66 to 90 N against the baselines' 22 to 28 N.
- Force capacity is not the limit either. The baselines pull 58 to 59 N on rigid ground; on sand their tension reaches 78 to 92 N for moments with the 15 kg cube (the robot swings against the elastic tether), and on softer sands they hold more: the RFT baseline 71 to 86 N for 10 to 31 s on 500/0.7 and 250/0.9 with 10 and 15 kg, the MJWarp baseline 45 to 75 N before it falls on light sand, 500/0.9 and loose poppy seed.
- The pull on sand does not follow the load: 22 to 28 N of horizontal foot force for 8, 10 and 15 kg alike on the stiffer sands, against 24 and 45 N for 8 and 15 kg on rigid ground. It follows the sand instead (higher where the foot sinks deeper). What the baselines learned on rigid or RFT ground, to answer a heavier cube with more push, does not carry over to MPM sand.
- One difference in traction does show: when the baselines' feet do reach a ratio of 0.2 to 0.3 they move back at 3 to 16 cm/s, where `her588wk`'s hold. The baselines' feet let go at a lower ratio than the MPM policy's. With the logs at hand this cannot be separated into posture (lean 6 to 8° against 13 to 21°), foot loading or timing; no contact pressure or joint torque was logged.
- The same policy shows the same behaviour on RFT sand it was not trained on (640, 0.73, 0.73): 39 to 41 N, feet moving back 2.8 to 2.9 cm per stance (42 to 78% of the sweep). The stall is not specific to MPM sand; it is what these policies do on a ground whose foot response differs from training, once something holds them back.

For the paper's sentence "because of the larger resistive force":

- (i) The object needs more force on MPM sand: supported. 8 kg: 42 to 47 N on the seven stiffer sands (42 to 56 N on all 12) against 32 N on rigid ground; 10 kg: 54 to 60 N against 40 N; 15 kg: 83 to 92 N against 59 N. The sand values are at a cube speed of 0.50 to 0.57 m/s (`her588wk` walks faster than commanded), the rigid values at 0.38 to 0.45 m/s. The baselines' own data agree: the 8 kg cube creeps at 0.03 to 0.05 m/s while the horizontal pull is 20 to 30 N and at 0.14 to 0.17 m/s at 40 to 50 N.
- (ii) The baselines do not deliver that force on sand: supported as a fact (29 to 48 N mean tension, cube at 0.01 to 0.13 m/s). It is not shown to be a limit of the sand's traction, and the data speak against that reading. It is a matter of what they learned: a response to load that works on rigid ground (and on the RFT training material) and does not appear on MPM sand.
- (i) alone does not explain the failure. The 10 kg cube on MPM sand needs 54 to 60 N, the same as the 15 kg cube on rigid ground (58 to 59 N), which both baselines tow in 16 of 16; on sand they tow the 10 kg cube in 0 of 16. The 8 kg cube on sand (42 to 47 N) needs about what the 10 kg cube needs on rigid ground (40 N). At equal force demand the baselines succeed on rigid ground and fail on sand. The cause to name is the pair: more resistance on the object, and less pull from the robot on sand.

**(b) The cube is not stuck at the start.**

- In every baseline trial on the sands examined (dense sand, beach sand, loose poppy seed; 8 and 15 kg) the cube moves 2 cm within 0.9 to 2.9 s. The tension when it first moves (0.2 s mean) is 45 to 52 N for 8 kg and 63 to 82 N for 15 kg, reached by the first jerk as the tether goes taut at walking speed. `her588wk`'s value at the same moment is 56 to 59 N (8 kg) and 95 to 98 N (15 kg), and its highest 0.2 s mean in the first 3 s is 63 to 66 N and 117 to 128 N, but these are the tensions of a robot accelerating the cube to 0.5 m/s in about a second, not a threshold the cube needs.
- There is no start-up peak that only `her588wk` overcomes. On dense and beach sand the baselines reach 48 to 57 N (8 kg) and 70 to 100 N (15 kg) again and again through the trial (highest 0.2 s mean per time window), and the cube moves each time; they do not sustain it. `her588wk`'s steady tension (42 to 50 N, 83 to 106 N) is below its own start-up values, so there is no sign of static resistance above sliding resistance in its trials.
- The 8 kg cube never stops for good: it creeps at 0.05 to 0.07 m/s from the first seconds to the end (0.11 to 0.14 m/s on loose poppy seed), 2 to 5 m in 40 s, moving 28 to 50% of the time. The 15 kg cube moves 0.3 to 0.5 m in jerks, 2 to 6% of the time.
- A stalled cube digs in: its centre ends 2.5 to 5.6 cm lower in the baselines' trials against 0.8 to 2.1 cm in `her588wk`'s. This may raise its resistance during the stall (on 500/0.7 the RFT baseline holds 71 N on the 10 kg cube, more than the 67 N at which `her588wk` tows it, and the cube moves at 0.11 m/s). ## Judgement

**(a) Measurement.**

- Sole reference: ankle-roll origin moved 3.5 cm down in the foot frame, world frame, so stepping in place does not by itself produce "slide". Slide is the backward displacement of that point between the first and last sample with vertical force above 165 N.
- Defect in the slide: the script projects the sole position on the robot's heading at each end of the stance (`along[a] - along[b]`, each with its own heading), so a heading change during the stance times the foot's lateral position enters as slide. With the heading held at its value at the start of the stance, the MJWarp baseline is unchanged (1.5 cm; correlation +0.03 to +0.11), but the RFT baseline's slide is 1.0, 1.1, 1.2 cm instead of 1.4 to 1.5, its rigid-ground slide 0.1 instead of 0.4 cm, and its correlation with peak force **+0.37, +0.31, +0.25** instead of +0.05, +0.04, -0.00. The top fifth of force then slides 0.8, 0.5, 0.4 cm more than the bottom fifth. The rule's condition (i) needs +0.3 and 1 cm, so it still fails, but "the slide does not grow with force" is wrong for the RFT baseline: it grows by less than 1 cm. (The same projection gives +0.55 to +0.59 on rigid ground for a slide of 0.1 cm, so the correlation alone means little; the size matters.) The zigzag of the dashed RFT line in the left panel of the figure (-0.3 to +1.1 cm) is this artefact.
- Sink is from the moment of loading, not from the settled surface. With the settled offsets in `settled_offset.txt` the lowest sole corner is already 1.5, 2.2, 4.2 cm below the settled surface when the load passes 165 N and 2.6, 3.9, 6.5 cm below at the end (MJWarp baseline; dense sand, beach sand, packed poppy seed; RFT baseline within 0.6 cm of this). "Sinks 0.9 to 2.2 cm" understates how deep the feet stand. The slide and the text should say "after the foot is loaded".
- 165 N is half the weight (344 to 358 N from the feet). A stalled robot in double support sits near this threshold, but the stances are not fragmented: 2 to 5% shorter than 0.2 s, none longer than 2 s, median 0.52 to 0.64 s.
- Stepping in place does make the slide hard to read as push-off slip. The stalled baselines' pelvis advances 2.1 to 2.7 cm per stance; the sole moves 1.0 to 1.5 cm backward and 0.9 to 1.3 cm sideways. It is a creep of a foot treading in its own pit, in all directions, not a backward slip under a push. Two further facts: (1) without the cube, walking at 0.41 to 0.44 m/s on the same sands with no net horizontal foot force, the same baselines' soles move back 0.7 to 0.9 cm and sink 2.3 to 4.1 cm per loaded stance; so about half of the slide and all of the sink are there without any push. (2) The comparison "sand against rigid at equal force" is stalled stepping against walking at 0.4 m/s; it compares gaits as well as grounds. The size of the difference (1 to 1.5 cm against 0.1 cm, 1 to 2 cm against none) is not in doubt.
- The analysis starts at 5 s, when the robot is already stalled. Looked at here: on the three stiffer sands the baselines move at 0.06 to 0.16 m/s in the first second and 0.05 to 0.09 m/s in the second; there is no phase of walking at speed that ends in a slip. On rigid ground they are at 0.39 m/s within the first second.

1. "When the tension rises they ease off and stand" is weaker than written.
