## What "force on the box" is

- **MPM:** the coupled solver's force on the box body from the sand (`foot_contact_force` of the environment, the linear part of the coupler's feedback for the box), read once per 0.02 s control step. The box is a 10 kg body on a PD gantry (1e5 N/m, 4e3 N s/m). During the steady window it lags the target by 1.5 to 1.6 cm in x and its underside is 6.07 to 6.10 cm below the probe's surface at 6 cm commanded. At the end of the 0.5 s hold after lowering it still presses on the sand with 243 to 329 N.
- **DEM:** `ContactAcc()` of the tracked box mesh times its mass (10 kg), averaged over 10 samples per 0.02 s. The box is kinematic. At the end of the hold the force on it is 1 to 6 N.
- **Seed-to-seed spread:** MPM 2.9 N (4.8%) across the three runs; DEM 1.4 N (1.9%). See issue 7 for what the MPM spread is.
- **Steadiness:** by thirds of the window, MPM voxel 3 cm gives -61.0 / -62.1 / -63.2 N; DEM A seed 42 gives -67.2 / -70.7 / -72.3 N (still growing by 7%); MPM voxel 4 cm gives -92 / -110 / -106 N. MPM Fz falls through the window (9.0 / 4.0 / 4.9 N at voxel 3 cm), so the "steady" Fz is a window mean of a drifting signal.

## Issues

1. **The box is at different depths in MPM and DEM, so the force ratio is not at equal depth.**
   Each simulator measures its "settled surface" differently: MPM takes the median of the highest particle centre in 4 cm cells in a 0.6 x 0.6 m window around the box start (`bench_probe.py` line 978; the write-up says "over the bed"); DEM takes the median over the bed of the highest grain top. One sits below the top of the material, the other above the mean surface. A common measure is the mass of sand above the box underside plane per unit area, before lowering, divided by the bulk density (mass-equivalent depth):

   | Run, 6 cm commanded | Sand above the underside [kg/m2] | Mass-equivalent depth [cm] | Fx [N] |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | MPM voxel 4 cm | 53.5 | 7.47 | -102.6 |
   | MPM voxel 3 cm | 47.9 | 6.52 | -62.1 |
   | MPM voxel 2 cm | 46.6 | 6.15 | -45.2 |
   | MPM voxel 1.5 cm | 46.2 | 6.07 | -37.6 |
   | DEM A, 20 mm | 37.9 | 5.15 | -80.3 |
   | DEM A, 15 mm | 38.4 | 5.22 | -70.1 |
   | DEM A, 11 mm | 39.6 | 5.39 | -65.7 |
   | DEM A, 9 mm | 40.0 | 5.43 | -61.0 |

   The MPM box at voxel 3 cm is 1.3 cm deeper than the DEM box (47.9 against 38.4 kg/m2, 25% more). Interpolating each simulator's own depth series to a common mass-equivalent depth: at 6.5 cm, MPM voxel 3 cm -62 N and DEM A -88.5 N (ratio 1.43); at 5.2 cm, -54.4 N and -70.1 N (ratio 1.29). For the 9 mm DEM run the ratio becomes 1.09 instead of 1.01. Consistent with this, the DEM grain-top surface is 0.8 cm above its mass-equivalent surface and the MPM centre surface is 0.5 cm below its own, and MPM moves 22% more sand (claim G). The write-up's caveat ("first force above 5 N at 0.4 cm and 0.0 cm above the surface") understates the offset; the DEM lowering trace has one sample per centimetre.
   The interpolation is rough for MPM because its force rises in steps with depth.
   *To fix:* define the surface the same way in both (for example the mass-equivalent surface, or the height at which the horizontally averaged density falls to half the bulk value), state the offset, and either rerun DEM at the matching depth (about 7.3 cm commanded) or report the ratio at equal depth from the existing depth series. Reword the summary: "agrees within 17%" and "ratio 1.01" are not supported.

2. **The MPM bed is not flat before the drag, which produces the reported MPM berm and most of the reported sinking.**
   Before lowering, the MPM surface within 28 cm of the side walls slopes down, to 1.6 cm below the bed median at the wall (voxel 3 cm; 0.9 cm at voxel 1.5 cm; DEM is flat within 0.2 cm). The "undisturbed sand" reference (|y| > 0.4 m) lies inside that slope. Cell by cell against the same run before the drag: the far field sinks 0.37 cm (MPM) and 0.00 cm (DEM); the MPM surface is lower than before everywhere across the track (no berm; -0.1 to -0.8 cm outside the trench), where DEM rises 2.3 cm at |y| = 22 cm; the trench is 8.0 cm below the sunk level in MPM against 5.75 cm in DEM. `height_profiles_main.png` shows this: the MPM cross profile never rises above 0.2 cm and falls to -2 cm at the walls. The MPM sand loses volume when sheared (mean particle height falls 0.27 cm over the run), the DEM sand gains volume (+0.07 cm). The 17 L "raised" in MPM is mostly the front pile and the reference offset.
   *To fix:* compute the deformation cell by cell against the map before the drag, replace "side berms 1.7 against 2.4 cm" with "MPM forms no side berm, DEM 2.3 cm", replace "sinks 1.5 cm during the run" with 0.4 cm, and state that the MPM bed is not flat near its walls. The takeaway line above `height_main.png` needs the same change.

3. **Claim C: the data supports "the MPM force is not converged in voxel size", but the factor 2.7 mixes resolution with depth.**
   Held fixed across the four voxel sizes, from the run JSON files: material preset, particles per cell edge (1.25), generated bed 2.4 x 1.2 x 0.25 m, total sand mass (460.8 kg), box, gains, path, speed, seed 42, solver preset `solver_cfg_all_particle` (sparse grid, `grid_type` not overridden), stack archive. Not held fixed:

   - settled bulk density: 717 / 735 / 759 / 761 kg/m3 (reported 721 / 737 / 763 / 766);
   - particle jitter: a fixed 0.05 m (plus or minus 2.5 cm), which is 0.8 particle spacings at voxel 4 cm and 2.1 at 1.5 cm;
   - sparse-grid node reserves (raised by hand at 2 and 1.5 cm; capacity only);
   - how much the bed is still sinking: far field -0.37 / -0.45 / -0.50 / -0.34 cm from before lowering to the end.

   *To fix:* report both by one definition and drop "within 1 mm".

With a threshold of 0.9 cm/s the MPM angles are 20 / 20 / 30 / 32.5 deg at 4 / 3 / 2 / 1.5 cm; with 2 cm/s they are 35 / 37.5 / 35 / 37.5 deg; DEM A stays at 32.5 deg for all three. Steps 20 to 42.5 deg have all 75 samples; the run failed during the 45 deg step after 60 of 100 samples, so "after all eleven steps had been recorded" is not exact and the 45 deg value (3.55 cm/s) is from a shorter window. The sliding angle is decided at 32.5 and 35 deg and is not affected.
   *To fix:* give the MPM sliding angle as a range (32.5 to 40 deg depending on the threshold) in the P1 table, and correct the sentence on the 1.5 cm run. The write-up already lists the ambiguous calibration target in its caveats; it should also be in the P1 takeaway.

The DEM seeds do give different beds. No two MPM runs with the same seed and settings exist to separate the seed's effect from nondeterminism.
   *To fix:* call them repeats, say what the seed changes, and state that MPM bed-to-bed variation was not measured.

## (a) What the MPM force is, and an independent cross-check

- **Buffer.** `bench_probe.py:963` reads `foot_contact_force[0, 0]` after each environment step (one physics step in round 4). That is the coupler's feedback for the box body, linear part (`streaming_mpm_env.py:308-312`), plus the rigid-floor contact force on the same body (`mpm_env.py:105-130`), which is zero here (box underside about 15 cm above the floor). The feedback is the sum over all grid nodes of the sand solver's collider impulse for the box, divided by the physics dt (`solver_implicit_mpm.py:6170-6200`): normal and friction parts, all faces, sand only. Gravity and the box's weight are not in it.
- **Cross-check: gantry force and box motion (done, passes).** Position, target and joint velocity are saved per physics step. In the five jumping-target runs the box velocity obeys `v[i] = c1 v[i-1] + c2 (target[i] - x[i-1]) + c3 F[i-1]` to a relative residual of 4e-6 (0.03 N), and `c2 / c3` returns the gantry stiffness exactly (3.000e6 and 1.000e5 N/m) with no scale fitted to the force. So the recorded number enters the box's equation of motion as a force in newtons, with the sign of the spring force, one step later. Using `c1`, `c2` from the jumping-target run of the same gantry and step, and the stiffness for the scale, the force implied by the spring and the motion in the constant-speed runs equals the recorded mean within **0.01 N in all 35 runs that have a matching calibration** (task 3 cm, tuned 4, 3, 2, 1 cm, both gantries; rms per-step difference 0.03 N). The spring carries about 62,000 N against the damper there, so this resolves 1 part in 1e6. Not covered: 1.5 and 0.75 cm (no jumping-target run at their step), and whether the impulse the solver reports equals the momentum the sand actually lost (internal to the solver).
- **Two things the records show that the write-up does not say.**
  1. The saved joint velocity is not the rate of the saved position. In the constant-speed runs the position advances at 0.5000 m/s while the joint velocity reads 0.4812 to 0.4988 m/s (0.4866 at 5 ms and 3e6 N/m; closer to 0.5 for smaller steps and softer gantries). Cause not identified. The 0.3% ripple holds for both measures, and the box is at 0.5 m/s by position. Which of the two the sand solver uses as the collider velocity was not determined; if it is the joint velocity, the collider is 0.2 to 3.8% slow, by an amount that changes with the time step and the stiffness, which would feed into the time-step and gantry sensitivities.
  2. Before the sand solve the box velocity has `dt F / m` removed with the box's own mass (`_rewind_mpm_proxy_bodies_kernel`, 10 kg), so inside the sand solve the box appears to respond as a free 10 kg body, not as a body held by a gantry (effective inertia 214 to 638 kg on the 3e6 N/m gantry, from the fit above). This is my reading of the code, not a test. It would explain the 5-fold force swing over one cycle. **The force was scanned against gantry stiffness but not against box mass**; one run with a 100 times heavier box would show whether the constant-speed force depends on it. The DEM box is kinematic.

## (e) Converged? Can a limit be extrapolated?

- **"Not converged" is right.** Steps of -12 / -17 / -15 / -10% from 4 to 1 cm and -9 / -5% to 0.75 cm; the spread between grid alignments (14 to 23%) is as large as one refinement step.
- **No honest extrapolation.** Fits of `F0 + C h^p` to the five means with their standard errors: p = 1 gives 69 +/- 4 N, p = 2 gives 93 N (rejected, chi2 8.4 on 3), free p gives 61 +/- 27 N with p = 0.9 +/- 0.4. Three-point Richardson gives 48 N (4, 2, 1 cm, p = 0.7) and 77 N (3, 1.5, 0.75 cm, p = 1.4); per alignment 35, 61, 83 N and one that is not monotone. The limit is somewhere between about 35 and 90 N. That the first-order fit lands on DEM A (71 N) must not be quoted as agreement.
- The time step is refined with the voxel (dt = voxel / 6 m/s) and is itself not converged (+4% per halving at fixed voxel, opposite in sign to the voxel trend), so the series is not a pure grid refinement.
