**2. "Feet sink under 1 cm in NeRD" does not hold as stated; the NeRD sand is rate dependent (item 4).**

- Sinkage against the rigid columns of the same run, NeRD (graph): 0.3 cm (`policy_nerd_16`), 2.1 cm (`trace_nerd`, same settings), 0.5 cm (64 environments), 1.6 cm (mine, 200 steps). With the early policies: 2.9 cm (NeRD policy, iteration 2,000) and 4.6 cm (MPM policy, iteration 3,000). MPM: 9.0, 9.0, 12.0, 13.1 cm.
- Per environment in `trace_nerd.npz`: the five walking sand robots hold the lower foot at +0.025 to +0.031 m (rigid stance: +0.021 m). The robot that stands the whole run (both feet loaded 99% of the time) sinks from +0.024 to -0.061 m in 5 s and stays there; a second one that stands 81% of the time reaches -0.068 m. In MPM all eight are at -0.045 to -0.067 m whether they walk or stand.
- So: for a stepping foot the NeRD sand acts as rigid ground; under a static load it creeps down about 8 cm. That fits a wrench built from effective masses (an impulse response with no static stiffness). The agent's new figure shows the two groups (second hump at -7 to -3 cm) without naming them.
- **Is 9 cm in MPM a NeRD error or MPM's known compaction?** The MPM side of the comparison ran NeRD's sand and NeRD's narrow jitter at the task discretization (voxel 0.04 m, 1.25 particles per cell edge, 25 iterations, 5 ms), the setting at which `FINAL.md` (T8) records sinkage 13 to 17 times DEM under loads up to 350 N by numerical compaction, and T3 records 65 to 88% of the sunk volume as compaction. T5 measured no density, so the share is **could not verify**, but 9 cm under a 35 kg robot on 1600 kg/m³ sand is in line with that finding and not with real sand.
- **NeRD's training data** (confirmed in `scripts/neural/scene.py`): MPM at the same discretization (voxel 0.04 m, spacing 0.032 m, 25 iterations, 5 ms), one sand (1600 kg/m³, friction 0.48, E and yield pressure 1e15 Pa), jitter 0.2 spacing. Loads: single objects; the 0.84 to 4.4 kg range is from the agent's reading of `dataset.py`, which I did not recompute. A foot carries about 350 N against at most 43 N there.
- **What the comparison shows, plainly:** under a humanoid the NeRD model as run does not reproduce the simulator it was distilled from. MPM sinks 9 cm, NeRD 0 to 2 cm when walking and 8 cm when standing. Neither value is validated against real sand; MPM's is probably too deep. The NeRD policy therefore trains on ground that is close to rigid for walking.

### Item 8: what the paper can and cannot say today

- **Can say:** Particle NeRD has been put into the mixed-terrain G1 task in place of MPM with the rest of the configuration identical, and a training is running. On one GPU the environment step is about 1.6 times faster and takes about a fifth of the memory, because rigid collision detection, not the sand, now dominates.
- **Cannot say:** any speed-up factor for training, that the NeRD policy learns faster, or that it transfers. The sand the policy trains on is near rigid for a walking foot, differs between graph and eager execution, and the one transfer test (iteration 2,000) fails in MPM. Sim-to-real has no result.
- **Before any "X times" goes into the contribution bullet:** (1) graph and eager NeRD agree, or the discrepancy is explained; (2) NeRD and MPM trained on the same GPU type and pool, same material setting (both fixed or both randomized), compared as time to the same score **measured in MPM**, not as time per iteration; (3) data-generation and network-training GPU hours counted (break-even, criterion 3); (4) rollout error against MPM under a foot-sized load (criterion 1), since without it the speed is that of a different ground; (5) more than one seed or a stated spread (MPM alone varies 19% between runs).
