### (a) Does keeping the links convex change the physics?

- **Feet against sand: untouched, confirmed.** MPM: the sand entry gets the bodies matching `FOOT_PROXY_BODIES` (`.../Robot/.*ankle_roll_link`, `g1_29dof/env_cfg/physics_cfg.py:37`) and its own bed and support bodies. NeRD: `collider_body_patterns=[r".*ankle_roll_link"]` (`g1_29dof_nerd/env_cfg/physics_cfg.py:74`), and the solver takes one primitive shape per collider body (`nerd_solver.py:194-205`, kinds box, sphere, cylinder, capsule only), so the NeRD driver never needed the conversion at all. The feet are boxes: with only `ankle_roll_link` shapes left colliding the model has 128 boxes for 64 robots and nothing else (`collision/only_feet.json`); none of the 22 hulls is on a foot body; the wrapper restores only shapes that were `CONVEX_MESH` and carry `/Robot/` in the label. Newton's implicit MPM would raise `NotImplementedError` on a `CONVEX_MESH` collider (`implicit_mpm_model.py`, `_get_shape_mesh`), and the MPM run with convex links ran, so no hull reaches the sand solver. The T5 session's statement (1) is correct.
- **Feet and the 4 cylinders against rigid ground: unchanged** (box and cylinder against mesh, same route). Without self-collisions the contact count is 222 with either link type (`no_self_collisions.json`, `convex_no_self.json`): while the robots walk, the ground contacts are the feet's.
- **Self-collision: affected.** The whole drop from 3,705 to 987 contacts is between links of the same robot: 3,483 against 765 (totals minus the 222 ground contacts). Mesh against mesh and convex against convex take different narrow-phase routes in Newton (`narrow_phase.py`), giving different contact points, counts and depths for the same pose. With convex links there are still about 12 link-link contacts per robot while walking, so some hull pairs are permanently within the contact margin. Whether any of them penetrate and carry force was not measured; if they do, the forces between links differ between the two set-ups and so does the robot the policies were trained on. ### 6. Is the margin needed, and the least invasive fix

- **For the sand, yes.** The foot plate is 1 cm thick and the voxel 2 to 4 cm; the sand solver reads the collider thickness from the same margin, so the sand sees a plate thickened by 2 cm. T3 recorded that a 0 cm margin on sand is "clearly worse" (2 resets in 10, more body tilt; `outputs/T3.md:3854`), but that run also removed the stop, so it does not say which of the two mattered.
- **For the rigid contact, no.** There it is an unintended second effect: the foot also meets the other foot, the shin and the rigid base 2 cm early.
- **Least invasive: filter the one pair per leg.** Two filter entries per robot, in the callback that already writes the margins or in the scene; nothing else changes, and the real links never touch. It leaves the other rigid effects of the margin (foot against foot, 3 to 8 loaded steps in a run; foot against the rigid base under shallow beds).
- **Cleaner, but touches shared solver code:** leave `shape_margin` at 0 and give the 2 cm to the sand solver only (`setup_collider(collider_margins=…)` exists in Newton; the IsaacLab driver does not pass it; T8's driver sets it after the build through `collider_materials(0)["margin"]`). The NeRD driver needs the same.

### C. Judgement

#### 1. The design, and what the two arms of the training-task test differ in

**What the counts can tell from chance.**

- **Episode ends, 8 robots against 8, one run.** Fisher's exact test (two-sided) reaches p below 0.05 only from a difference of 5 robots (7 against 2, 6 against 1, 5 against 0, 8 against 3). The observed differences are 3, 4, 3, 1, 1, 0, 0 (p = 0.31, 0.13, 0.31, 1, 1, 1, 1). Paired by robot (same robot, tile and, within the MPM arm, same task), a sign test needs 6 robots that differ all one way; the most seen is 4 of 4 (10,000, cap 25; p = 0.125). No cell separates the two policies on episode ends.
- **The slack of 2 of 8** therefore fails a difference of 3 that chance produces one time in three, and passes a difference of 0 to 2 that is compatible with very different true rates (4 of 8 has a 95% interval of 16 to 84%). "Does not carry over" at 5,000 and "carries over" on ends at 15,000 and at the end are not findings; they are readings of one draw.
- **Velocity error.** One mean over 8 robots x 500 steps per cell; no per-robot values on file, so no interval can be computed. Scatter seen in the data: the same policy at the two caps differs by 0.2 to 6.1%; the NeRD-trained policy in MPM goes 0.245, 0.255, 0.192, 0.225 over the checkpoints. The margins at 15,000 (4% inside, 1% outside) are below that; the 1.79 to 1.85 at the end is above it. What holds across cells: from 10,000 on the NeRD-trained policy's velocity error on MPM sand is 1.3 to 1.85 times the MPM-trained policy's in 6 of 6 cells.
- **Goals of 10 trials.** A difference needs 5 of 10 (10 against 5, p = 0.033). 10 of 10 excludes a failure rate above 31% (95% interval 69 to 100%), and less than that says, because the trials are near copies: within a cell the 10 speed shares lie within 0.01 to 0.02 of each other, and the 5 robots of a run share a settled bed.
